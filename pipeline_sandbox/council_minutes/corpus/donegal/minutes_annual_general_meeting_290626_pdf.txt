C/189/26 
ELECTION OF THE CATHAOIRLEACH  
Cllr Canning, the outgoing Cathaoirleach welcomed members to the 
meeting together with the Doherty and Crossan families who were 
present in the chamber. 
 
He proceeded to invite nominations for the position of Cathaoirleach for 
the period June 2026 to June 2027. 
 
Cllr Nic Mheanman proposed Cllr Gary Doherty for the position of 
Cathaoirleach. 
 
Cllr Doherty, she said, had served on the Council since 2013 and was a 
proud republican and  Sinn Féin representative whose politics were rooted 
in equality & social justice and in service to the community to the best of 
his ability. He had, she confirmed, consistently worked to help ordinary 
families and to ensure that this county received its fair share of investment.  
He was someone, she added, who understood clearly the need for social 
democracy, and respected the mandate of all elected members. 
 
This, she said, was a proud day for the Doherty family and for the Sinn Féin 
party and all who had supported him throughout his political career. 
 
Cllr Mc Monagle seconding the proposal noted that Cllr Doherty was an 
experienced Councillor with strong community roots and beliefs. He was, 

4 
 
he said, someone who had served the community well and had on a 
number of occasions served as the Cathaoirleach of the Lifford/Stranorla 
Municipal District and therefore had the experience and capability to be 
the first citizen of this county.  
 
As there were no other nominations, Cllr Gary Doherty was  elected 
Cathaoirleach for the period 2026-2027. 
 
C/190/26 
ELECTION OF THE LEAS-CATHAOIRLEACH 
Cllr Doherty proceeded to invite nominations for the office of Leas 
Cathaoirleach. 
 
Cllr A Doherty proposed Cllr Terry Crossan for the position of Leas- 
Cathaoirleach. He said that he was a hard working Councillor for the 
Inishowen area and would be an able assistant for the Cathaoirleach over 
the coming year. 
 
Cllr Murray seconding the proposal said that Cllr Crossan was a fair and 
decent man and also a passionate and dedicated Irish republican with a 
deep interest in the Irish language. He would, he said, carry out the role 
with dignity and that he was honoured to be associated with his 
nomination.  
 
As there were no other nominations it was thus resolved to elect Cllr 
Terry Crossan as Leas Cathaoirleach for the period 2026-2027. 
 
The Cathaoirleach proceeded to address the meeting and thanked his 
fellow councillors for the honour bestowed on him. It was a privilege, he 
said to be chosen by your peers to serve as the first citizen of this county, 
and thanked members for putting their trust in him. 
 
He paid tribute to the previous Cathaoirleach, who, he noted, had been 
an excellent representative for the county. Thanks, was also extended to 
Cllr Donal Mandy Kelly who had ably assisted the Cathaoirleach over the 
past year.  
 
He congratulated the Leas Cathaoirleach, Cllr Crossan on his 
appointment noting that he had been a lifelong republican who had 
strived to work for the betterment of all. 
 
Cllr Doherty thanked Cllrs Nic Mheanmean and Mc Monagle for their 
endorsement.  
 
Members, he noted, brought a range of views and life experiences to the 
chamber and said this was what local democracy was about, creating a 
forum where each member had the right to be heard. Going forward, he 
said that he would strive to treat each member with respect and courtesy 
and would make the office as accessible as he could.  
 

5 
 
He was, he advised, a committed republican whose belief in reunification 
was as strong today as it had been when he joined the Sinn Féin party in 
1999. He said that he was guided by the principles of social justice, 
equality and progressive reform and that politics was about improving 
people’s lives and raising the standard of living for all.  
 
It was important, he added, that the message went out that Donegal was 
about its people, its communities, the culture, the sporting organisations 
and the Diaspora.  
 
Today was, he confirmed a very proud day for his family, and he paid 
tribute to his wife Louise and his children, Dara, Aoife & Conor all who 
had been a great support to him in his political career.  
 
He thanked his sisters, Lisa & Michelle together with his extended family 
circle for their ongoing support.  
 
Cllr Doherty also paid tribute to his late mother and father who, he said, 
would have been incredibly proud today of his achievement. 
 
Concluding, he referenced the need to highlight mental health issues and 
to get the message out there that adversities can be overcome. It was a 
huge honour, he stated to represent this county, and he would, in the 
coming year represent the people of Donegal to the best of his ability. 
 
Cllr Paul Canning thanked the Chief Executive, the Senior Management 
Team, Council Staff and the Elected Members for their support over the 
past year noting that he had been ably assisted over the past year by the 
former Leas-Cathaoirleach, Cllr Donal Kelly.  
 
Cllr Kelly also extended his congratulations and noted that Cllr Canning 
had represented Donegal County Council to a very high standard over 
the last 12 months.  
 
The Leas-Cathaoirleach, Cllr Terry Crossan said that it was honour and 
privilege to accept the position and extended his thanks to Cllrs A 
Doherty and Jack Murray who had nominated him for the position.  
 
Members of the various groupings then offered their congratulations and 
assured the new Cathaoirligh of their support. They acknowledged also 
the excellent work carried out by Cllrs Canning and Kelly during their term 
in office.  
 
The Chief Executive on behalf of the Senior Management Team and the 
staff of Donegal County Council assured Cllr Doherty and Cllr Crossan 
that they would have the full support of all staff in the organisation and 
said that he was looking forward to working with both in the year ahead. 
 
This concluded the business of the meeting.
