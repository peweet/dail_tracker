NOTICES OF MOTION 
 
Notice of motion No. 1 
 
Notice of motion in the name of Cllr. D. O’Brien (proposer), seconded by Cllr. A. Flynn 
Kennedy (5th August 2024) 
 

‘That this Council adopt Part 4 of the Public Health Amendment Act 1890 to include all 
Municipal Districts. Part IV of the Public Health Acts Amendment Act 1890 is a statutory 
provision for the local regulation (by licensing) of public Music and Singing events. 
 
Where Part IV has been adopted all Music and Singing events are required to be licensed and 
the District Court has jurisdiction to hear License Applications. An Garda Siochana and the 
Fire Officer are Notice Parties to such applications. 
 
While it is believed that Part 4 is in place and functioning as expected across the County 
there is a potential anomaly in Bray which might represent a legal question mark. The 
passing of this Motion will confirm, endorse and validate the regular status of the Act as it 
pertains to Music and Singing license provision and is in the interest of both providers and 
patrons.’ 
 
Response: 
 
Legal advice on this matter was received and is as follows: 
 
In the absence of the evidence required by Section 3(6) of the 1890 Act that Bray Township 
Commissioners or Bray UDC or Bray Town Council adopted a resolution under Section 3 of 
the 1890 Act, it cannot be assumed that Part IV of the Public Health Acts Amendment Act of 
1890 has been adopted for the Bray Municipal District. 
 
 
 
Part IV is at present partially adopted across the County .There is evidence Part IV was 
adopted by the Wicklow County Health Board in 1931 and  Wicklow and Arklow Urban 
District Council’s in 1932. 
 
It is proposed to list this item as an agenda item on the December agenda to consider and 
adopt Part 4 of the Public Health Amendment Act 1890 for the County to include all 
Municipal Districts. 
 
Notice of Motion No. 2 
 
Notice of motion in the name of Cllr. C. Winstanley (proposer), seconded by Cllr. D. Alvey 
(27th August 2024) 
 
‘That this council will disregard the half-rate Carers Allowance in full, the Disability 
Allowance received by children aged between 16 and 18 in full, and the Carers Allowance at 
the basic rate of social welfare when calculating the rent of Council tenants under the 
Differential Rent Scheme (DRS). The Council notes that there are plans for a national review 
of the DRS, however, this has yet to be published. The Council will implement changes to its 
DRS based on this motion, and again when the national differential rent plan has been 
published. 
  
Differential Rent Scheme Paper - https://familycarers.ie/media/3122/differential-rent-
scheme-and-caring-households-submission-to-the-department-of-housing-local-
government-and-heritage-on-the-review-of-the-differential-rent-scheme.pdf 

  
You’ll see from Table 1 on page 4 that rent differs greatly across the country, and even more 
so in Wicklow. The case study household is charged €127.50 in Bray, but outside of Bray they 
are €90.50 per week.’ 
Response 
 
Section 58 of the Housing Act 1966 authorises local authorities to determine the rent 
charged on local authority properties.  The autonomy of local authorities to set and collect 
rents is subject to broad principles including that rent levels should be based on income and 
the tenant’s ability to pay the rent.  This flexibility allows local governments to use 
discretion when deciding which sources of income are included or disregarded for rent 
assessment purposes. 
 
Each local authority operates and manages its own Differential Rent Schemes and the 
provision of same is an executive function. 
 
Local authority rents are calculated as a proportion of the principal earners income together 
with a contribution from any subsidiary income(s) in the household.  There are nuances and 
multiplicity of circumstances that can apply in each local authority scheme.  A fundamental 
consideration for all local authorities in relation to the Differential Rent Scheme are 
vulnerable households.  The application of the Differential Rent Scheme is a wide-ranging 
subject and there are many cohorts of vulnerable households for example Travellers and 
disabled people, households on working family payment, households in receipt of social 
welfare payment only and any geographical variations that may exist do not relate to caring 
households only. 
 
In the submission by Family Carers Ireland in June 2023 to the Department of Housing, Local 
Government and Heritage on the review of the Differential Rent Scheme an example was 
provided in a hypothetical family to demonstrate the different approaches to the setting of 
rents and geographic variations.  The example provided does not accurately reflect all 
factors that could impact on the local authority assessment across the country and another 
scenario may result in a different outcome. 
 
It is important to note that any change to a Differential Rent Scheme needs to be considered 
in the totality of it impact and should not be done in isolation as there may be unintended 
or indirect negative consequences. 
 
It should also be noted that while local authorities assess each individual household in line 
with their operational Differential Rent Scheme based on the individual circumstances of 
the household.  In exceptional circumstances where payment of a rent calculated in 
accordance with the scheme would, in the opinion of the local authority, give rise to 
hardship, the local authority has discretion to accept a lesser sum from a tenant for a 
specified period. 
 
Outside of the above it is recommended that support for carers would be better addressed 
at national level where it can be considered in the context of all other more vulnerable 
cohorts and households.  While a standardized, national local authority social housing rent 

model including the provision of comprehensive guidelines would ensure a consistent 
approach it would need to provide a mechanism to address on a permanent basis any 
reduction in income experienced by local authorities.
