suspension of standing orders to discuss the following motion:
'Re: Review of Court Facilities and Resource Allocation for County Wicklow
PREAMBLE: WHEREAS the population of County Wicklow has reached approximately
150,000 residents, the current judicial infrastructure is insufficient to meet the legal and
administrative needs of the growing community.
MOTION: That the Members of Wicklow County Council request the Executive to formally
seek from the Minister for Justice and the Courts Service of Ireland a time-bound capital
investment plan to address the significant disparity in judicial resources allocated to County
Wicklow.
GROUNDS FOR MOTION:
1.
2.
3.
4.
Population vs. Infrastructure: Despite a population of 150,000, County Wicklow is
currently served by only one operational Circuit Court and two District Courts.
Comparative Inequity: County Louth, with a lower population of approximately
139,000, possesses superior judicial infrastructure, including:
a. Two fully functioning Circuit Court venues (Dundalk and Drogheda).
b. Three District Court locations.
Capital Investment Discrepancy: Note is taken of the significant investment in
neighbouring regions, such as the €9 million courthouse delivered in Drogheda in
2017, highlighting a lack of equivalent capital expenditure in Wicklow.
Access to Justice: The current deficit in operational venues within Wicklow risks
causing delays in the administration of justice, increasing travel burdens for legal
professionals and citizens, and straining existing facilities.
ACTION REQUIRED:
2
That the executive requests a formal review of the Courts Service's capital plan to ensure
that courthouse provision in County Wicklow is brought into alignment with its demographic
requirements and is at least commensurate with that of comparable administrative counties.

Suspension No. 2
Cllr. P. Leonard, seconded by Cllr. W. O'Toole and supported by Cllrs. P. Fitzgerald, M. Murphy,
S. Bourke and P. Kennedy requested a suspension of standing orders to discuss the following
motion:
That Wicklow County Council formally writes to the Taoiseach, the Tånaiste, and the
Minister for Health seeking urgent Government intervention in the case of Arklow teacher
Ms. Eimear Byrne, who requires life-saving surgery that can only be carried out in the United
States.
This Council acknowledges and commends the extraordinary generosity and community
spirit shown by the people of Arklow and the wider public, who have already raised in excess
of €160,000 towards the estimated €600,000 cost of Ms. Byrne's treatment.
Given the critical and time-sensitive nature of Ms. Byrne's condition, and the serious risk
posed by any further delay, Wicklow County Council calls on the Government and the HSE to
urgently examine all available avenues of support to ensure she can access this essential
surgery without delay'.
It was agreed by all to have a discussion on the topic at 4.30 p.m

ITEM NO. 15
To note Annual Financial Statement (AFS) 2025 and approve by resolution additional net
expenditure in excess of budget in accordance with Section 104(7) of the Local
Government Act 2001 (ii) transfers to and from capital.
Mr. B. Gleeson, Director of Services gave an overview of the Annual Financial Statement
2025 as follows:
Wicklow County Council's overall financial position remained strong throughout
2025.
The total expenditure incurred in the revenue account last year was €174m,
generating a small surplus of €47,000 after transfers. The overall cumulative surplus
at the end of the year is just under 50,000.
Capital account expenditure includes transfers and was € 140m in 2025 which
represents an increase of over €26m on capital spend in 2024.
The vast majority of this expenditure was under our housing programme.
Commercial rates retained its collection level of 93%, despite the reduction in the
vacant relief rate from the 16% to the 20% last year.
Housing rents collection rate has increased from 84% to 87%, and housing loans has
increased from 80% to 85%.
