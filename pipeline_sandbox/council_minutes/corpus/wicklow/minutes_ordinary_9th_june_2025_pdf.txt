ITEM NO. 8 
 
To consider and adopt Wicklow County Council Flag Policy. 
 
Ms. H. Purcell, Meetings Administrator advised the Elected Members that Wicklow County 
Council receive a number of requests during the year to fly different flags.  It is proposed to 
put a policy in place.  The policy has gone before the Senior Management Team and 
Corporate Policy Group (CPG) in June.   
 
The Corporate Policy Group asked that the policy would come to the full Council for 
discussion and for agreement.  
 
A lengthy discussion in relation to paragraph number 7, "Other Flags" took place.  In 
particular the Pride flag and the flag of Palestine and whether or not they should be 
included in the policy or whether they should be covered by "no guest flag will fly for more 
than seven days in any month or more than once in any year".   
 
A broad discussion followed in which the Elected Members raised queries and concerns. 
 
Cllr. J. Behan proposed, seconded by Cllr. T. Fortune to re-establish the Protocol committee.  
It was agreed to list this as an agenda item at the July meeting. 
 
It was proposed by Cllr. P. O’Brien, seconded by Cllr. M. Corrigan ‘to consider and adopt the 
Wicklow County Council Flag Policy with the addition of the line ‘and brought to full Council 
for approval’ at the end of paragraph 11 policy review which will only apply in relation to 
new requests for flags. The current flags, Pride flag for the month of June and the Palestinian 
flag to be reviewed monthly, remain as they are and are outside this policy’   
 
was put to a vote and following a roll call was passed by a margin of 25 votes for, 2 against, 
4 not present and 1 abstained viz:-

Notice of motion number 2 
 
Notice of motion in the name of Cllr. C. Winstanley (proposer), seconded by Cllr. D. Alvey 
(8th May 2025) 

8 
 
 
 
‘That Wicklow County Council, in the interest of public safety and accessibility, creates a bye-
law requiring all waste collection companies operating within the County to ensure that their 
employees, when collecting and emptying bins, replace the bins back to the location where 
they were found—typically against a wall or boundary—and in a manner that is safe and 
does not pose a hazard to visually impaired individuals or others with disabilities. 
It is noted that bins left obstructing footpaths or placed haphazardly after collection can 
create significant dangers for people with visual impairments and other mobility challenges. 
By enacting such a bye-law, the Council would enhance safety and accessibility for all 
residents. 
We understand that the Council would need to formally adopt a bye-law to enable it to 
require bin collection companies to comply with these safety measures. This proposal is 
inspired by a similar bye-law adopted by Dún Laoghaire-Rathdown County Council, and we 
request that Wicklow County Council consider implementing a comparable regulation’. 
Response 
 
Wicklow County Council note the concerns raised in this notice of motion.  
 
In light of Cllr. Winstanley’s Notice of Motion the Climate Environment Recreation and 
Amenity Directorate when reviewing all Waste Bye-Laws which is scheduled for Q.3 2025 
will amend the Bye-Laws where possible to align with Cllr. Winstanley’s suggestion and 
alleviate the issue of bins left obstructing footpaths and in so doing eliminate the danger 
that this poses for people with visual impairments and other mobility challenges.  
 
Draft versions of the new Bye-Laws will go on public display and will then be brought before 
the Climate Action Biodiversity Environment Energy Marine Recreation and Amenity 
Strategic Policy Committee for noting ahead of a full council meeting final approval.
