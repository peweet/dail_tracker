Dréacht Plean Oidhreachta Chill Chainnigh  - Draft Kilkenny Heritage Plan 
Cathaoirleach, Cllr. Mary Hilda Cavanagh invited Ms. Regina Fitzpatrick, Heritage 
Officer to present the Draft Kilkenny Heritage Strategy. 
 
Ms. Fitzpatrick advised that over the past 13 months the Heritage Office prepared a 
Draft Heritage Strategy for County Kilkenny.  The work was guided by the County 
Kilkenny Heritage Forum and informed by extensive consultation with the people of 
Kilkenny and the County’s Heritage Stakeholders.  To begin she played a short video 
asking the people of Kilkenny what Heritage meant to them. 
 
Ms. Fitzpatrick extended her thanks to the Elected Members for their consistent 
support, encouragement and assistance.  Special acknowledgement was given to the 
Co-Chairs of the County Heritage Forum Cllr. Maria Dollard and Cllr. Fidelis Doherty. 
 
She explained that the Strategy was developed in line with the process set out by the 
Heritage Council and formal thanks were also expressed to the Heritage Council for 
their guidance with particular recognition of Virginia Teehan, Chief Executive. 
 
Key insights from developing the strategy included: 
 
▪ 
Kilkenny’s heritage is central to our identity as a people. 
▪ 
It is what makes our County distinctive, authentic and rooted in place. 
▪ 
Every town, village and townland has its own unique heritage character. 
▪ 
Heritage is a core reason people choose Kilkenny as a place to live, work, visit and 
invest. 
▪ 
Recording what is at risk of being forgotten. 
▪ 
Providing access to heritage as a shared right. 
▪ 
Conserving what matters deeply to people. 
▪ 
Maintaining the threads of memory in everyday landscapes. 
▪ 
Embedding heritage in decision making across the Council’s services and in 
community life. 
▪ 
Enhancing awareness of the value, importance and relevance of heritage in our 
everyday lives. 
 
The strategy includes plans to celebrate heritage through initiatives such as the 2027 
European Year of the Normans working in partnership with neighbouring counties 
Wexford and Waterford on a regional approach to commemoration.  Contains 53 
actions which will be delivered through an annual Implementation Plan. 
 
Ms. Fitzpatrick thanked the Members for their time and asked that they would 
consider adopting this Strategy.

Nuashonrú maidir le Tuilte - Update in relation to Flooding 
Ms. Mary Mulholland gave an update in relation to Flooding events from 
January to February this year as follows: 
 
▪ 
Drone footage was used to record flooded areas and help identify where 
floodwater was coming from. 
▪ 
Severe flooding occurred in Thomastown and Inistioge.  
▪ 
Civil Defence and County Council staff went door to door to identify 
properties affected by floodwaters. 
▪ 
37 domestic properties were impacted across Graiguenamanagh, Inistioge, 
Thomastown and Callan. 
▪ 
46 business premises and public amenity areas were affected. 
▪ 
Following a meeting with the Minister interim flood relief measures were 
recommended. 
▪ 
Proposals for acqu-dam or bram infrastructure at Graiguenamangh, 
Thomastown, Inisitoge, Freshford and Callan. 
▪ 
Sought funding for pumps and brams, CCTV surveys, individual property 
barrier equipment. 
▪ 
Submission to Minister came to  just over €1 million. 
▪ 
17 roads were closed and 69 roads were damaged. 
▪ 
A funding application was submitted to the Department of Transport in 
February with an estimated repair cost of over €5 million.  This included: 
o 
29 roads in the Callan–Thomastown area 
o 
20 roads in Castlecomer 
o 
20 roads in Piltown 
The highest cost was in the Callan–Thomastown area at €2.4 million 
including works required at the Quay in Thomastown. 
▪ 
A separate submission of €55,000 was included for damage to bridges. 
 
Ms. Mulholland acknowledged and commended the staff who worked 
tirelessly to keep roads open, carry out repairs and reopen major routes as 
quickly as they could.  She acknowledged the communities affected and 
thanked them for their patience and cooperation stating that there was strong 
engagement and cooperation with local communities throughout the event. 
 
The Cathaoirleach thanked Ms. Mulholland, staff and the communities who

Cllr. Deirdre Cullen acknowledged the Minister’s response in relation to 
Kilkenny Schools Campus. She said that the situation at Kilkenny City 
Vocational School should be considered urgent, noting that the school currently 
has no electricity due to adjacent building works. She highlighted that 28 
students are still required to walk into the City twice a day for mobility breaks. 
She explained that many of the students experience social and emotional 
disadvantage and are already dealing with trauma, which the school works 
hard to address and support and the lack of proper facilities is adding to that 
trauma and preventing the school from providing the environment the 
students deserve. She called for fairness and equity in education so that every 
student in Kilkenny can reach their full academic potential regardless of 
background.  
 
She also said she was happy to hear comments from the Tánaiste indicating 
that additional funding will be announced and asked that Kilkenny be part of 
that funding and awaits an outcome for meeting with An Taoiseach. 
 
Cllr. Seán Ó hArgáin said he was concerned with the response received stating 
that children are being unfairly impacted after being promised a new school.  
He said that years of underinvestment in special education are unacceptable 
and asked for a commitment to progress the project in the coming months is 
honoured, stressing that the community cannot wait another five years. He 
noted that student numbers are already increasing and said the school must be 
delivered as soon as possible.
