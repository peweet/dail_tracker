C/139/26 
 DONEGAL TEN-T PRIORITY IMPROVEMENT PROJECT AND ROAD 
SAFETY PRESENTATION 
 
 
 Mr Bryan Cannon, Director Roads & Transportation welcomed Mr 
Damian Mc Dermott, Senior Engineer, N.R.D.O, Ms Cliodhna 
Campbell, Senior Engineer, Roads & Transportation together with Ms 
Pamela Smullen, Road Safety Officer to the meeting. 
 
 
 
 He informed members that the application for approval under the 
Roads Act 1993 (as amended), Environmental Impact Assessment 
Report (EIAR), and Compulsory Purchase Order (CPO), for the TEN-T 
Priority Route Improvement Project Donegal would be published in 
national and local newspapers on the 14th May 2026 with the public 
consultation process starting on the 14th May also. This, he said, was a 
very significant step for the project which is of regional and national 
importance. It was noted that it represented the single greatest 
investment in transport infrastructure in Donegal in the modern history 
of the county and was vital for the future growth and development of 
the county. Documentation would, he confirmed, be available on the 
Donegal County Council website, An Comisiún Pleanála Website with 
hard copies available in the County House, Lifford and the various 
Public Service Centres countywide. He thanked members for their 

 
4 
 
support in getting the project to this stage and noted the hard work and 
dedication shown by the National Roads Office whose collaboration 
with the T.I.I, had brought the project to this stage.  
 
 
 
 
 
 Cllr Mc Gowan noted at this juncture that a specific format had been 
agreed for discussing roads related issues. He said it was essential that 
the Area Roads Managers and the Roads Overseers were brought to 
these special meetings and proposed that a schedule for each 
respective Roads area be agreed on and the relevant personnel invited 
to attend each dedicated meeting so that everyday issues could be 
highlighted and dealt with, and feedback sought. This was seconded by 
Cllr Bradley.  
 
 
 
 The Director of Service noted that several topics for discussion through 
the Special Meeting process had been agreed by the Corporate Policy 
Group and said that if there were other areas of concern then these 
could be looked at. 
 
 
 
 The Cathaoirleach confirmed that this proposal to amend the format 
and schedule of Roads topics could be discussed by the CPG at their 
next meeting on the 18th May. 
 
 
 
 Members welcomed and acknowledged the progress on the TEN-T 
highlighting the fact that it was a gamechanger for the county.  
 
 
 
 Members then received a presentation on the various aspects of road 
safety and the measures being employed to promote same throughout 
the county.  
 
 
 
 Ms Cliodhna Campbell, Senior Engineer introducing the presentation  
alluded to the National Safety Strategy and the funding utilised to 
improve the protective quality of our roads and associated 
infrastructure. She proceeded to outline the status of the DOT Low 
Cost Safety Improvement Schemes and an analysis of same. 
Reference was made also to the Safe Routes to School Scheme and 
works ongoing in relation to same. Data on the Safe Speed’s initiative 
was also provided with the aim of reducing speeds to safe and 
appropriate levels for the roads concerned and road users themselves. 
Attention was also drawn to the need to improve road user standards 
and behaviours in line with traffic legislation supported by enforcement.  
 
Mr Damian Mc Dermott, Senior Engineer, gave an update on the 
various types of safety schemes being progressed county-wide 
including those constructed to date, those due for completion in 2026 

 
5 
 
and those at assessment stage.  
 
Ms Pamela Smullen, Road Safety Officer, outlined the work being 
carried out by the Donegal Road Safety Working Group and its role in 
the production of the Donegal Road Safety Plan 2022-2030. 
She provided an update on the ongoing activities and initiatives being 
progressed by DRSWG and Donegal County Council supported by the 
Road Safety Authority. These, she said, were all designed to influence 
and guide driving behaviours with the aim of saving lives and reducing 
serious injuries. Attention was drawn to a number of road safety events 
and campaigns undertaken including the Roadsafe Roadshow.  
 
Concluding, Ms Campbell drew attention to the work being undertaken 
to promote safe and healthy modes of travel, including safe work 
related road use. She said that safety concerns raised by members 
would be assessed initially by the Area Roads Team but that the first 
priority was to address these through discussion or low cost 
enhancements at MD level.  
 
It was noted that where such concerns could not be addressed at local 
level the Area Engineer would arrange to have a road safety 
assessment form completed and would submit same to the Road 
Design Safety Team so as to determine whether a funding source was 
available or if the item could be included with a future application 
for funding.

Mr Damian Mc Dermott, Senior Engineer noted that a significant 
number of issues had been raised and said that responses would issue

set and that this would commence for an eight week period beginning

the planning process.
