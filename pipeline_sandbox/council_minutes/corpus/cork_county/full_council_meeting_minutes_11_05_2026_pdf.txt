ACCESS OFFICER  
 
  15/5-1 
Cllr Ann Bambury proposed, seconded by Cllr Ger Curley 
 
“That Cork County Council would clarify the following: 
o Does Cork County Council have an Access Officer? 
o If so, what is the defined role and parameters of the Access Officer 
 
And will ensure the following: 
o That at least one Access Officer is in a full-time dedicated position (not spread across 
other roles) 
o The Access Officer will engage closely with the Housing Directorate, personally assist 
those seeking housing support, train staff to recognise when someone requires more 
assistance and how to give that assistance. 
o The Access Officer’s contact details will be shared with disability groups, councillors 
and the public.” 
 
Members noted the response from Angela McAllen, Assistant Chief Executive, as follows: 
Response:  
 
Cork County Council has a process in place whereby an Access Officer is employed within the County 
Hall Complex. Any queries regarding access outside of the complex are forwarded to the relevant 
Municipal District Offices and specific directorates for their action.  
 
Cork County Council also employs a dedicated Disability Liaison Officer to support staff with 
disabilities. 
 

This role includes: 
• 
Providing ongoing support to employees with disabilities. 
• 
Assisting the Recruitment Team in the recruitment of candidates with disabilities. 
• 
Engaging with external organisations on matters relating to the recruitment and employment of 
people with disabilities. 
 
Cork County Councils Library & Arts Department provides a very significant accessible approach to 
the delivery of its services, through physical access, stock selection, ICT & online services, age friendly 
strategies, sensory resources. A range of staff training is provided to ensure an awareness of the diverse 
needs of library users. 
 
In addition, Cork County Council promotes inclusion & access through the L.C.D.C. 
Cork County Council is reviewing its approach to access within the overall organisation and will 
provide an update to members when concluded.

WASTEWATER ENFORCEMENT 
 
 21/5-1 
Cllr Audrey Buckley proposed, seconded by Cllr Sheila O’Callaghan 
 
“That Cork County Council provide a report outlining the total number of open planning enforcement 
and wastewater-related enforcement cases across the county, including the length of time each case 
has been active and focus more on enforcement going forward to ensure more timely resolution of 
such cases.” 
 
Members noted the response from Michael Lynch, Assistant Chief Executive, as follows: 
Response:  
 
All planning and wastewater related enforcement files are subject to individual assessment and 
consideration of relevant matters before determination; in a manner that reflects legislative requirements 
to ensure due process is followed. Matters vary from file to file and can include a range of complex 
issues including planning and environmental law, private property and third-party rights and can also 
involve overlapping areas of responsibilities with colleague State Agencies/Govt Dept in the form of 
interagency collaboration/co-operation. In this context every Case File is different, and timeframes vary 
depending on the particulars of each case. 
  
Cork County Councils planning enforcement service is informed by the County’s extensive 
geographic scale, the complexity of individual cases, and the multi-stage statutory requirements of the 
Planning & Development Act and Regulations, and legal requirements of the Judicial System. 
Planning enforcement services are delivered by a dedicated enforcement section that operate in close 
coordination with staff across the Directorate and wider organisation, as well as with external State 
Agencies / Government Departments as may be appropriate. 
 
Cork County Council is currently managing 1,576 open planning enforcement cases, all of which have 
been subject to investigation, assessment and the issuing of Warning Letters.  
 
In the two-year period 2024-2025 after Cork County Council reconfigured enforcement services, 549 
new planning enforcement cases were opened - 79% of which have been resolved following detailed 
investigation and assessment, while approx. 15% have progressed to the service of Enforcement Notice. 
Twenty-one of those cases are currently being prosecuted through the Courts, while a further fifty cases 
are under assessment following engagement in the form of retention planning applications or Section 5 
Declarations, in response to the initiation of enforcement. 
 
 
In relation to water quality enforcement, wastewater cases are identified through a combination of 
Councils work programmes (including Section 4 licence monitoring, the National Inspection Plan for 
domestic systems), and the investigation of water pollution complaints. 
 
Currently, Cork County Council is engaged in 384 active wastewater enforcement or non-compliance 
cases, including court prosecutions. Such matters are managed by a dedicated team of Environmental 

Officers and Scientists who prioritise proactive stakeholder engagement and technical support to 
achieve environmental resolution, in line with Cork County Councils Air, Water & Laboratory Services 
Compliance and Enforcement Policy. 
 
During this discussion, the members made the following points: 
• 
Wastewater enforcement is an important issue. 
• 
The number of open planning enforcement cases is significant. 
Michael Lynch, Assistant Chief Executive responded as follows: 
• 
Services have been reconfigured. 
• 
79% of files have been closed. 
• 
The more significant files are being progressed. 
• 
Cork County Council have been very proactive in this area.
