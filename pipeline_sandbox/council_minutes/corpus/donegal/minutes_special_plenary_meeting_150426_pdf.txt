C/127/26 
HOUSING CAPITAL UPDATE  
 
 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer, Housing Capital, briefed 
 Members in relation to the various updates from the previous meeting 
on the 11th March 2026. An overview was also provided in respect of 
projects completed in the period 2022 -2026 together with an update on 
projects currently on site combined with an update on the status of  
Projects being progressed through the competitive dialogue process. 
Ms Claire Burke, Senior Executive Architect gave an update on projects 
at planning and detailed design stage. 
 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer then responded to a number of 
queries and advised of the following:- 
 
That the issue of electric vehicle charging points and associated park-
ing was being looked at with consideration being given to the use of 
ducting as a means of future proofing schemes. 
 
In terms of broadband provision, he confirmed that ducting was pro-
vided in social housing developments and noted the comments raised 
by Cllr O’ Fearraigh in relation to the new development in Doire Beag. 
 
He said that the Council were continuing to look at two sites in 
Gweedore for development and were assessing same in terms of their 
viability for social housing. He acknowledged the comments made in 
terms of overall parking requirements at various sites throughout the 
county. 
 
Mr Gallagher acknowledged the comments made in relation to the 
housing need in Gleneely, Malin and Carndonagh and said that the 
Housing team were aware of the importance of same. 
 
Mr Patsy Lafferty, Director Housing, MD Services & Capital Projects 
referenced the Approved Housing Body development in Carndonagh 
and said that members were aware that the process under the CALF 
Scheme was that the AHB applied directly to the Department for fund-
ing with the Local Authority providing specific detail to assist with that 
application.  
 
He said that there had been a meeting with Department officials the 
previous day and that it was hoped to meet CHI again to share details 
in relation to the overall requirements. He said that members would be 
kept updated as the matter progressed. 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer, responding to Cllr Kelly’s queries 
on water and sewerage capacity together with future capability at  
Ballymacool, stated that there was ongoing consultation with Uisce 
Éireann  in relation to this matter. He said that there had been confir-
mation of the feasibility of the project at Ballymacool from Uisce 
Éireann. 

5 
 
He also confirmed that the design team were working on the compila-
tion of a set of draft tender documents and that these had to be re-
viewed before the project could go to tender and works commenced. 
 
In relation to Cllr Devine’s query on archaeological testing at  
Ballymacool, he said that this was an important aspect and that there 
was ongoing testing at initial design, pre-planning and planning stages. 
This, he advised, allowed issues to be dealt with upfront and that it was 
not anticipated that there would be any problems prior to the contractor 
arriving on site.

C/129/26 
UPDATE IN RESEPECT OF THE CAPITAL PROJECTS DELIVERY 
UNIT  
 
 
 Mr Brendan O’ Donnell, Senior Engineer, delivered a presentation on 
the work being progressed by the Capital Projects Delivery Unit. An 
update was provided in relation to the following: 
 
• Fort Dunree 
• Letterkenny Surs Phase 1 
• Lifford Common 
• Riverine Community Park 
• Repowering Buncrana  
• Creeslough Regeneration  
• Ailt an Chorráin Harbour Development Phase 2 
• Carndonagh Tus Nua 
• Ballybofey/Stranorlar SEED Project 
• Ramelton Reimagined 
• Letterkenny 2040 – Market Square 
• Letterkenny Courthouse 
 
Details were also provided in relation to a number of new projects that 
had been identified. Concluding, he informed members that the Capital 
projects Delivery Unit had moved to the Housing, MD Services and 
Capital Projects Directorate and that the Community Capital Team 
were also moving to the CPDU. 
 
Members welcomed and acknowledged the comprehensive 
presentation raising several queries. 
 

6 
 
Mr O’ Donnell in response advised of the following:- 
➢ That there was no guarantee that Failte Ireland would fund the 
project but that it was to be welcomed that they were prepared to 
fund the new planning application. He said that €5m in match 
funding would need to be in place.  
➢ Work, he advised, was ongoing in terms of an RRDF application 
for the Garda Barracks in Buncrana. 
➢ Other options were being looked at, he said, in relation to the 
proposed Pedestrian Bridge from the Amazing Grace Park to the 
Pier at Buncrana given the complexities involved and the fact 
that it was in a Special Area of Conservation.  
➢ Progress on the Alpha Beta buildings, he noted, was dependent 
on the discussion with the funders.  
➢ The Market Square Project in Letterkenny, it was acknowledged, 
a slow burner and that there was no funding in place for advance 
works. He said that there were positive updates emanating from 
the Department and that as soon as funding was available a 
contractor would be on site. It was noted that the tender 
concerned was now a year old and that there could be pricing 
differences at this stage. 
➢ An update on the Glencar project could be provided at a future 
meeting. 
➢ Finner Fire Station, he confirmed, would go to tender in May 
2026 but that it was unlikely that works would commence in 
2026 or 2027. The planning process, it was noted had started 
and that this would be done in parallel to the detailed design 
work. An update, he stated, would be provided at MD level. 
Funding, he said, had been awarded but was not enough and 
that same was being discussed with the Department. 
➢ There was acknowledgement also of the queries in relation to 
Magheroarty, Rann na Feriste and Arranmore. Mr O’ Donnell 
confirmed that these were primarily being looked after by the 
Piers and Harbour team and that the issues raised would be 
brought to their attention.  
➢ Members were informed that the CPDU were willing to address 
and present updates to any Council committee including Coiste 
na Gaeilge under the direction of the Senior Management Team. 
➢ The resubmission of the planning application in respect of Fort 
Dunree, it was noted, required additional work by the design 
team on the proposed effluent treatment system. Confirmation 
was also provided that some of the ecological surveys 
necessary were seasonally sensitive and that as a result it would 
not be possible to provide a dedicated timeline for the 
submission of the revised planning application given that it had  

7 
 
to be comprehensive and robust. It was noted that further 
information on the timelines involved could be presented to the 
Inishowen Municipal District members. 
➢ Approval for the Carndonagh Tús Nua project it was confirmed 
would be submitted to the Department by the 17th April 2026. 
➢ Commitment given that focus would  be made on the funding 
aspect for the delivery and maintenance of playgrounds. 
➢ Comments in relation to the publicising of projects to be taken on 
board together with the publication of the schematics involved in 
new and developing projects.  
 
Cllr Mc Gowan asked for a breakdown of the €5m match funding 
referenced in the presentation and as to where these monies were 
sourced from. It was important, he said, that the public were aware that 
the Council did not get 100% grants and that match funding was 
needed to progress capital projects.

the following:- 
 
➢ That comments in relation to the publicisation of the various 
schemes would be taken on board and followed up on. 
➢ The refurbishment of farmhouses could still be pursued under 
the vacant property grant scheme and that funding for a 
conservation grant scheme report had just been opened up with 
maximum funding of €5000 applicable in this instance. It was 
noted that details were available on the website. 
➢ Contact would be made with the SEAI in relation to the provision 
of presentations at MD level. 
➢ With regard to the vacant above the shop scheme, it was 
confirmed that  
➢ any commercial unit concerned could be registered in a 
company name and could remain vacant or be occupied. There 
was a requirement however, she added, that the residential unit 
above the shop be in an individual name.  
➢ The use of a “Notice of Enquiry” had proved beneficial in tracing 
possible ownership of vacant properties.   
 
Cllr Mc Mahon was informed that he would be contacted directly in 
relation to several derelict properties in his own area. 
 
Cllr Carr asked if there had been any update on his motion as 
submitted to the March Plenary meeting requesting that the Minister for

disappointment that a presentation on the plan for social housing 
remediation had not been included on the agenda today.
