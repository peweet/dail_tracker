ITEM NO. 6 
 
To consider report on the Strategic Infrastructure Development application in respect of 
Ballinclare Quarry. 
 
The Chief Executive advised that the Elected Members were circulated with the report of 
the Chief Executive on the Strategic Infrastructure Development application. 
 
Ms. L. Earls, Director of Services advised the Elected Members of the steps involved in the 
process.  In accordance with the Planning & Development Act, the Members may, by 
resolution, decide to attach recommendations, specified in the resolution to the report of 
the Chief Executive 
 
Cllr. P. Leonard proposed a resolution to be attached to the report of the Chief Executive on 
Ballinclare Quarry as follows: 
 
‘Proposed wording on behalf of the members to be included as an appendix to the Chief 
Executive Report on Ballinclare Quarry, alongside the minutes and transcript of the full 
council discussion on the report. 
 
ABP Case Reference: PA27.321255 
Description: Ballinclare Quarry, Proposed Material Recovery / Recycling Facility and Inert 
Landfill 
 
This summary outlines the key objections to the proposed development at Ballinclare 
Quarry and highlights discrepancies in the Chief Executive’s Report. 
 
ENVIRONMENTAL SECTION 
 Groundwater 
Claims of "no change in well levels during dewatering" are unsubstantiated. 
Dewatering began in 2022, but groundwater monitoring only started in 2024, after 
pressure had already reduced. Uisce Éireann warns the backfill could disrupt 
groundwater flow, levels, and quality, impacting domestic and agricultural wells. 
 Waste Acceptance Criteria 
Kilsaran proposes extending waste intake beyond inert soil, aiming to increase 
contaminant limits in line with EU Council Decision 2003/33/EC. In 2023, a similar 
EPA application sought to triple limits for Sulphates, Chlorides, Arsenic, and other 
contaminants, raising risks of elevated pollution levels. 
 Wastewater 
Contradictions arise in Kilsaran’s statements: they claim no hazardous leachate will 
be generated due to the inert nature of the waste, yet plans include a leachate tank. 
No provisions are made for leachate or landfill gas monitoring, leaving potential 
contamination risks unaddressed. 

 Untreated Surface Water 
Runoff from the site will flow untreated into Kilmacurragh Stream and Potter’s River 
via aswale. This poses a long-term contamination risk, with no monitoring plan for 
potential leachates dispersing into local water networks. 
 Quarry Lining 
The proposed clay liner may include filter cake, a by-product of washing 
contaminated materials. Using potentially polluted filter cake risks lining the quarry 
with harmful substances. 
 Invasive Species 
Despite assurances against accepting soils with invasive species, Kilsaran intends to 
apply for EPA approval to dispose of soils containing species like Japanese Knotweed 
and Giant Hogweed. A similar application was rejected by ABP in 2023. 
 
ROAD SAFETY 
 Reduced Road Safety 
Kilsaran’s own consultations admit the proposed road widening (to 6m) may 
encourage higher speeds, reducing safety on the L1157. The design is inadequate for 
two HGVs (2.9–3m wide) to pass safely, leaving no margin for error, increasing 
accident risks. 
 N11/M11 Traffic Congestion 
The addition of 600–750 HGV movements weekly will exacerbate existing congestion 
on the already overburdened N11/M11 corridor. 
BIODIVERSITY 
 Peregrine Falcons 
Proposals fail to protect breeding peregrine falcons. Recommended buffer zones for 
nesting raptors are 200–600m, but the application allows only 15m in cliff height. 
Such proximity to human and mechanical activity jeopardizes the falcons’ survival, 
critical for maintaining the species in the region. 
 Special Areas of Conservation (SAC) 
The Natura Impact Statement (NIS) fails to conclusively address potential impacts on 
water-dependent habitats in the Buckroney Brittas Dunes and Fen SAC. Hydrology 
and leachate studies contain uncertainties, and increased waste acceptance criteria 
heighten risks. The NIS lacks the necessary assurances required by law. 
HUMAN HEALTH 
 Asbestos - The site contains asbestos, yet mitigation plans are insufficient. Questions 
remain about testing protocols, air and water safety measures, and whether the site 
is even suitable for development. 
 There has been scant mention of the asbestos discovered onsite in 2016, which was 
distributed to over 20 building sites before being returned. Since the site closure, the 
quarry has filled with water, minimising the airborne risk of asbestos particles. 
 Dewatering the quarry and proposed activities could release asbestos particles into 
the air, posing severe health risks to local families. 
 Despite being a critical issue, asbestos is mentioned only briefly in the 2,000-page 
proposal and relegated to the acronym NOA (Naturally Occurring Asbestos). A 
comprehensive, independently reviewed plan to mitigate these risks must be 
enforced before considering approval. The lack of transparency on this long standing 
issue has eroded trust with local residents. 

Conclusion 
 
This development poses severe environmental, safety, biodiversity, and health risks, 
particularly concerning asbestos contamination, groundwater impacts, and insufficient 
safeguards for wildlife. Furthermore, the lack of meaningful consultation and public 
engagement throughout this process necessitates an Oral Hearing to ensure transparency, 
and address public concerns. In the light of the genuine and legitimate concerns of local 
residents and the many issues specified above, we the Elected Members of Wicklow County 
Council request An Bord Pleanála to refuse permission for this SID application’.
