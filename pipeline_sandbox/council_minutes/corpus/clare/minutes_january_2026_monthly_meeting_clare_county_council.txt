Item 12: Group Water Schemes
Ar moladh Cllr. A. O’Callaghan
Cuidithe ag Cllr. M. Shannon agus glacadh leis
“Given the continued prominence of Group Water Schemes across the county and the ongoing issue of billing for water quality testing, that greater collaboration between the local authority and Irish Water be undertaken to provide support and, where possible, accelerate the process of taking these schemes in charge.”
(M)
T. Mellett, A/Senior Engineer, Climate Action, Environment and Water Services replied as follows:
“The Taking in Charge of Group Water Schemes on to the public network is at the sole discretion of Uisce Eireann as the National Water Authority. Clare County Council through its Rural Water Team is responsible for administering the Multi-Annual Rural Water Programme 2024-2026. In Clare 33 Group Water Schemes have been allocated €24m funding for infrastructure upgrade works through the Programme. This will allow the schemes to meet Uisce Eireann’s threshold for Taking in Charge. The Rural Water Team guide Group Water Schemes through this process and meet with Group Chairperson’ and Secretary’s to advance their projects. The Rural Team communicate with Uisce Eireann in order to ensure schemes are advanced in line with Uisce Eireann processes and to avoid unnecessary delays.
The water quality testing of a Group Water Scheme is carried out by our Environment Laboratory team, the parameters and frequency of testing are overseen by the EPA and are covered by the Drinking Water Regulations 2023. Clare County Council is obliged to report the results of this testing programme to the EPA in February of each year. The cost to Group Schemes of this testing can be recouped as part of the annual subsidy application process. Any Group Scheme needing assistance with the application should contact the Rural Water Team for assistance.”
The elected members commended the Water Services team and acknowledged the significant voluntary effort involved in managing Group Water Schemes (GWS). Members noted that many schemes are fully compliant with all criteria yet remain untaken in charge. They sought clarity on the billing process, highlighting concerns that some GWS secretaries are now elderly or deceased, which requires review if substantial bills are being issued. Members also raised the issue of leaks that need to be addressed and queried whether all staff have now been reassigned to Uisce Éireann. In addition, members requested that the previously planned workshop with Uisce Éireann be rescheduled, as it would provide an opportunity for constructive engagement.
The Cathaoirleach, Cllr. P. Murphy confirmed that the workshop with Uisce Éireann is being arranged and is expected to take place in February.
Dr. C. Kirby, Director of Service, advised that there are currently 196 Group Water Schemes, with 84 receiving water from Uisce Éireann. She noted that the EPA sets the testing parameters, but the associated costs to the Group Schemes can be recouped through the local authority. Dr. Kirby further advised that overlap testing had been identified and rectified, and follow-up will be undertaken to ensure that the listed secretaries remain the correct contacts.

Item 16: Legislation Regarding Short Term Lets
Ar moladh Cllr. B. Slattery
Cuidithe ag Cllr. agus glacadh leis
“
That Clare County Council would provide the elected members with a detailed briefing on the impact of the current planning and rent pressure zone legislation for those people who plan on operating a short term let for the 2026 tourist season.”
(M)
Helen Quinn, Senior Planner, Planning, Economic, Rural & Community Development replied as follows:
“The General Scheme of the Short-Term Letting and Tourism Bill 2025 aims to regulate short-term lettings in Ireland establish a national register and ensure compliance with EU Regulations (EU 2024/1028 and EU 2028/1724). The legislative provisions provide for full compliance with the EU Short Term Rental Regulations from 20
th
May 2026.
The Short-Term Letting & Tourism Bill (2025) provides for the following:
All properties offered for short-term letting (up to 21 nights) must register with Fáilte Ireland.
Hosts will receive a unique registration number, which must be displayed on all listings (e.g., Airbnb).
Online platforms (Airbnb, Booking.com) must verify and display this number. Non-compliance can lead to fines of up to 2% of annual turnover for platforms.
A Rent Pressure Zone (RPZ) is an area where legal restrictions apply to a rent increase.  Rent Pressure Zones that apply in Co. Clare are as follows;
Ennis LEA is a designated RPZ since 31
st
Aug 2023
Shannon LEA is a RPZ since 30
th
Nov 2023
Killaloe, Ennistymon and Kilrush LEA’s are designated as RPZ’s since June 2025
The Residential Tenancies (Amendment) Act 2025 came into operation on 20 June 2025, which extended the operation of Rent Pressure Zones (RPZs) to cover the entire country.  The extension of Pressure Zones to a nationwide rent control system was one of the recommendations in the
Review of Rent Pressure Zones Report
, completed by The Housing Agency on behalf of the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage in April 2025.    It is one of a suite of reforms to the rental sector to address the current housing crisis across the country.
As part of the measures to help address pressures in the private housing rental market, planning legislative reforms to regulate the short term letting sector were provided for under the Residential Tenancies (Amendment) Act 2019 and supplementary regulations made by the Minister for Housing, Planning and Local Government entitled the Planning and Development Act 2000 (Exempted Development) (No. 2) Regulations 2019 which state that
“The use of a house or part of a house situated in a rent pressure zone for short term letting purposes is a material change in use of the house or part thereof”
.
In effect this regulation means that a material change of use occurs where a residential property is put into short-term letting use, such that it requires the benefit of a planning consent.  Any property owners who intend to operate a short term let in the 2026 tourist season, or thereafter, should engage with the Planning Authority to establish what planning consent requirements they may have ahead of the season.  Failure to have appropriate planning consent in place ahead of letting will result in unauthorised development taking place and potential enforcement action.”
The elected members noted the significant implications that the proposed legislation on short‑term rentals would have and expressed concern about the lack of clarity regarding its implementation timeline and operational details.
Members highlighted that many parts of Clare are heavily dependent on tourism and that short‑term lets play a vital role due to the limited availability of hotel accommodation. They stated that the proposed legislation is not suitable for Clare in its current form and suggested that its introduction should be deferred for two years to allow homeowners adequate time to prepare.
The members also queried how the legislation would apply to individuals renting a room within their own home and emphasised that a ‘one‑size‑fits‑all’ approach is not appropriate in this context.

Item 18: Affordable Housing Scheme
Ar moladh Cllr. J. Crowe
Cuidithe ag Cllr. J. Garrihy agus glacadh leis
“At the moment there are two areas in Clare i.e. Ennis & Shannon in the affordable housing scheme, I am proposing that going forward all future housing developments within Clare are included in this scheme, there will be the requirement of a communication to be sent to the office of the Minister for Housing, James Browne.”
(M)
Pádraig MacCormaic, Senior Executive Officer, Housing Services replied as follows:
“Members will be aware that under the original eligibility criteria for Affordable Housing Clare did not qualify as the HNDA (Housing Needs and Demand Assessment) conducted by the Department of Housing Local Government & Heritage (DHLGH) for the county as a whole did not meet their affordability constraint threshold. However, following a detailed submission to the DHLGH, Clare County Council received approval in principle to submit detailed applications to the fund for specific developments. Our submission was primarily based on a Pilot Study on Housing Needs & Demand Assessment with a particular focus on affordability challenges in the towns of Ennis and Shannon.
This was completed in March 2023. Subsequently the Department announced that they would welcome proposals for settlements outside of the larger urban areas.
Clare County Council continues to seek expressions of interest in relation to developer led proposals, in Ennis and Shannon, and throughout the county. Developer proposals received will be reviewed by Clare County Council prior to submission to DHLGH. Any proposed development will require its own
definitive Scheme Proposal and Business Case to be submitted to the DHLGH. While each scheme will have its own unique set of circumstances and will be assessed on its own merits, schemes, as they are designed and developed, must continue to meet the criteria set out in the Affordable Housing Act 2021, including meeting the affordability challenge. In this regard we are currently engaging with a number of developers in relation to potential Affordable Schemes outside of Ennis and Shannon.
The DHLGH have indicated that they will require LA’s to undertake housing need demand assessments to inform the next housing delivery action plan relating to social and affordable delivery. We await the greater detail of the DHLGH requirements, but we expect that the assessment and the plan will relate to the entire county.”
The elected members acknowledged that a cohort of individuals have incomes that are above the eligibility threshold for social housing but insufficient to purchase a home on the open market. They emphasised the need for the provision of affordable housing to address this gap. Members further noted that the current income band for affordable housing should be expanded in light of prevailing house prices. It was also highlighted that ensuring a balanced mix of housing options is essential to attracting investment into the county.
It was proposed by Cllr. G. Keating, seconded by Cllr. J. Killeen and agreed by the members present that the time be extended to 6.30p.m. in order to complete the business of the meeting.

Item 20: Finance and Budget Workshop
Ar moladh Cllr. R. McInerney
Cuidithe ag Cllr. P. Hayes agus glacadh leis
“That Clare County Council establish a quarterly finance and budget workshop for Elected Members, to be facilitated by the Executive, with the purpose of:
·
Providing Councillors with a clear and accessible comparison between the adopted annual budget and actual expenditure and income to date, including variances, emerging pressures and underspends;
·
Reviewing projected outturns for the remainder of the current financial year, with early identification of risks, cost escalations or savings opportunities;
·
Supporting early and informed preparation for the following year’s budget, including horizon-scanning for known policy changes, capital commitments, staffing pressures and funding uncertainties;
·
Strengthening transparency, democratic oversight and collective understanding of the Council’s financial position throughout the year.
Such workshops would be held on a non-statutory basis, in addition to existing budgetary processes, and structured to enable constructive engagement between Members and the Executive in advance of key budget decisions.”
(M)
N. Fitzgerald, Director of Service, Finance, Investment & Enterprise replied as follows:
“The Executive welcomes the objective of strengthening transparency, democratic oversight and collective understanding of the Council’s financial position throughout the year.
Clare County Council operates a comprehensive statutory and governance framework for financial oversight, including:
·
Monthly Management Reports to council. Finance prepares quarterly reports to the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage. Report on Revenue, Capital and Collections (RCD) and General Government Balance (GGB). These reports are national requirements for local authority finance. This involves a detailed look at current expenditure compared to budget across all directorates. These submissions are included in the monthly management report to elected members in the month following their submission to our parent department.
·
Half Year Financial Report- a detailed overview of the financial position of the council as of June each year, is issued to all elected members. It is then an agenda item at the September council meeting for the attention of elected members and the executive address any queries.
·
Annual Budget process adopted by Elected Members in accordance with the Local Government Acts; This involves significant financial briefings to the Corporate Policy Group and two budget briefings to the full council.
·
Monthly management reporting budget holders, tracking income, expenditure and variances.
·
Annual Financial Statement and audit, prepared and published in line with national requirements and the Public Spending Code; These are presented to members for their review and adoption in line with legislation.
The level of annual expenditure now more than €300million and the vast level of service delivery has brought some complexity into the local government finance model and the proposal for workshops to explain the finance model to elected members is needed.
The proposed workshops would be non‑statutory and are intended to complement—not duplicate—the above processes. The proposal for quarterly workshops is supported in principle, however the Executive notes that the Council’s spend profile is not evenly distributed across the year. Traditionally Expenditure profiling is not balanced across each quarter and income collection does not follow an even pattern. For this reason, workshops held early in the year may offer limited insight or meaningful variance analysis.
In addition, the spend is across in excess of 1,000 services and 2,100 job codes . There is a resource implication of providing quarterly workshops to the level outlined in the motion in addition to meeting the statutory finance reporting requirements as outlined above. To maximise value for Members and ensure efficient use of resources, the Executive will commence with a commitment to a workshop Mid-Year with the following scope:
·
Review the Annual Financial Statement of the prior year as completed, presented to members and subject to audit.
·
Review the Mid-year financial result that will be in draft preparation. Focus on budget‑to‑actual comparisons; variances; emerging pressures and underspends; projected outturn for year‑end.
·
Highlight Emerging Financial Demands
This workshop can address the objectives as set out in the motion and in addition supports early discussions in advance of the following years budget process
The Executive therefore recommends that the first such workshop be scheduled after June, when mid‑year data provides a clearer and more informative picture of expenditure patterns, emerging risks, income performance and projected outturns. Subject to the Council’s agreement, the Executive will schedule a workshop in Mid-2026 and decide thereafter on whether additional workshops are required while avoiding parallel reporting structures or unnecessary duplication.”
The elected members noted the significant level of investment currently being made in the County and acknowledged that dedicated workshops would be beneficial in enhancing their understanding of ongoing initiatives. The members agreed that it is their responsibility to be fully informed regarding the financial aspects of these developments, and that such workshops would support them in fulfilling their duties and enable them to make well‑informed, collective decisions.
