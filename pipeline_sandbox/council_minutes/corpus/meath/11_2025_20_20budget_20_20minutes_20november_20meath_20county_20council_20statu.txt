1. To consider Chief Executive’s Draft Budget of Meath County Council for Financial Year 
ending 31st December 2026 
 
The Cathaoirleach opened the meeting, and it was noted that the Statutory Budget book 
including Tables A-F had been circulated to Councillors in advance of the Preliminary Budget 
meeting.  
 
Chief Executive, Mr. Kieran Kehoe, addressed the meeting and noted that Budget 2026 
provides for expenditure of over €258 million, which is an increase of 11.5% on the 2025 
budget.   
 
He noted that Meath County Council boasts one of the highest commercial rates collection 
rates in the country, and a modest adjustment to the ARV as outlined in the Budget Report,

will help maintain Meath’s competitiveness while facilitating essential infrastructure and 
regeneration projects.  He further noted that by prioritising the protection of smaller 
businesses and reinvesting in local needs, this increase will enhance Meath’s economic 
resilience and foster community development for years to come.  It was noted that a core 
principle behind the proposed adjustments is to protect smaller ratepayers from any 
financial burden. Specifically, as part of a business support scheme, there will be no increase 
for businesses with commercial rates below €20,000, ensuring that small enterprises, the 
backbone of our local economy, are adequately and fully protected. The aim is to create a 
stable environment for these businesses, allowing them to thrive and contribute to the 
community.  
 
The proposed increase in commercial rates is expected to generate an additional net 
revenue of €4.6m annually. This new income will enable targeted investments in priority 
areas that directly benefit both communities and businesses.  In particular, €1.5m per year 
will be earmarked for improvement and maintenance of local roads, representing a 
significant additional €6m investment over the next four years beyond the existing annual 
roads programme.  Furthermore, €0.6m annually, or €100,000 for each municipal district, 
will be allocated to fund traffic safety improvements, equating to an additional €2.4m over 
the next 4 years. 
 
Moreover, a substantial portion of the funds, €2.5m per year, will support a transformative 
€10m regeneration programme over four years, aimed at enhancing town centres and rural 
communities throughout Meath.   
 
He further outlined his key focus areas for 2026 as follows: 
 
• 
Putting a particular focus on our local county roads, including maintenance and 
enhanced safety measures. 
• 
Ensuring Meath County Council continues to enhance our community and 
amenity infrastructure, particularly in our growing urban centres. 
• 
Supporting towns and villages to drive rural regeneration and stimulate economic 
and social regeneration in town centres. 
• 
Establish Meath as a leader in job creation and set a benchmark for economic 
development. 
• 
Delivering the objectives of the Government’s Action Plan on Housing Supply and 
Targeting Homelessness and the Meath County Housing Strategy, including age-
friendly housing. 
• 
Deliver the ambitious goals outlined in our Climate Action Plan and in our nine 
decarbonising zones. 
• 
Enhancing and maintaining our services, to ensure the consistency and continuity 
in delivery.

Finally, the Chief Executive thanked the Elected Members for their continued support, the 
Management Team for their contributions and support and the Director of Finance and the 
Finance Team for the preparation of the Budget.   
 
Ms. Fiona Lawless, Director of Finance, made a presentation on the Draft Budget for the 
Financial Year ending 31st December 2026. The presentation included information on: 
• 
Analysis of income and the various income sources; 
• 
Overview of commercial rates and a profile of rate payers; 
• 
Analysis of Expenditure, including operational and administrative aspects. 
 
The presentation also outlined the Annual Budget 2026 by Service Division, with notable 
increases in service areas highlighted, as follows: 
 
• 
Service Division A – Housing and Building 
• 
Service Division B – Road Transport and Safety 
• 
Service Division C – Water Services 
• 
Service Division D – Development Management  
• 
Service division E –  Environmental Services 
• 
Service Division F –  Recreation and Amenity 
• 
Service Division G – Agriculture, Education, Health and Welfare 
• 
Service Division H – Miscellaneous Services 
• 
Service Division J – Central Management Charges  
 
The Director of Finance concluded by highlighting the service areas that would see the most 
increases – Housing, Transport, Economic Development and Urban and Rural Regeneration, 
but stated that all Departments would see an increase in expenditure. 
 
Following the presentation, the Cathaoirleach thanked the Chief Executive, Director of 
Finance, Management Team, and the Finance Team for their work in preparing Budget 2026.  
There were Interventions by Councillors Ronan Moore, Gerry O’Connor, Mike Bray, Padraig 
Fitzsimons, Joe Fox, Maria Murphy, Sarah Reilly, Nick Killian, David Gilroy, Brian Fitzgerald, 
Maria White, Eugene Cassidy, Sharon Tolan, Alan Lawes, Yemi Adenuga, Emer Toibin, 
Peter Caffrey and Dave Boyne. 
 
The Elected Members broadly welcomed the proposed budget, which many contributors 
considered to be positive and progressive, and there were expressions of appreciation for 
the manner in which the Budget was compiled and how the Council’s budget is managed 
generally.  The protections afforded to small businesses in the County were welcomed, as 
were the budget increases for Housing, Roads, and Road Safety measures within Municipal 
Districts.  Some concern was raised regarding the increase in commercial rates and the 
budget allocation for Housing and Roads.

3. Subject to section 6 of the Local Government Rates and Other Matters Act 2019 as 
amended and in accordance with the Budget adopted, determine the annual rate on 
valuation applicable in the calculation of the amount of the rate under Section 4 of the 
Local Government Rates and Other Matters Act 2019 as amended. 
The motion proposed by Councillor Gerry O’Connor and seconded by Councillor Mike Bray 
was agreed by a show of hands vote with 31 members in agreement, 3 members not in 
agreement and 3 abstentions. 
 
 
4. 
That Meath County Council makes a scheme for the abatement of rates due to it by 
liable persons, or classes of liable persons, in respect of vacant properties in 
accordance with the provisions of Section 9 of the Local Government Rates and Other 
Matters Act of 2019 for the financial year ending 31st December 2026: 
 
100% of rates due where rates demand is less than €100,000 
10% of rates due where rates demand is between €100,000 and €249,999 
 
0% of rates due where rates demand is over €250,000 
 
Unanimously adopted on the proposal of Councillor Nick Killian and seconded by Councillor 
Gerry O’Connor. 
 
 
5. That Meath County Council approves of the transfer to capital account of the sums

Unanimously adopted on the proposal of Councillor Sarah Reilly and seconded by Councillor 
Francis Deane. 
 
 
6. That Meath County Council approves the estimated over expenditure for financial year 
31st December 2025 as set out in Table A – F and outturn details. 
Unanimously adopted on the proposal of Councillor Mike Bray and seconded by Councillor 
Peter Caffrey. 
 
 
7. To adopt the Business Support Scheme, 2026 in accordance with Section 66 of the Local
