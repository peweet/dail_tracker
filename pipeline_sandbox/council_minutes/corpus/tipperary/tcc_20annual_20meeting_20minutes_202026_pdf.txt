Item No. 1.1 
Welcome 
 
Mr. Sinéad Carr, Chief Executive, Mr. Liam Brett, Mr. Brian Beck, Mr. Jonathan 
Cooney, Mr. Mark Connolly, Mr. Damien Ginty Directors of Services.

The Cathaoirleach, Cllr. John Carroll welcomed everyone to the Annual Meeting 
of Tipperary County Council and to Burgess GAA Club and thanked the 
Committee sincerely for allowing the facility to be used for the meeting and for 
the warm welcome extended.   
The Meetings Administrator took a roll call and commenced the meeting with 
the opening prayer. 
The Cathaoirleach noted the retirements of Ms. Evelyn Harty Corporate 
Services, Nenagh and Mr. John Walsh, Caretaker Clonmel Civic Offices and a 
small presentation was made to them in recognition of their service. 
Cllr. John Carroll, in his final address as Cathaoirleach of Tipperary County 
Council, expressed his sincere gratitude to Leas Cathaoirleach, Cllr. Mark 
Fitzgerald, the elected members, Chief Executive, Council Management and 
staff for their support and cooperation throughout his term.  He reflected on 
the honour of serving as First Citizen of Tipperary and highlighted the 
importance of fairness, respect and strong local democracy. He also 
acknowledged the outstanding contribution made by communities, volunteers, 
sporting organisations and local groups across the county. The Cathaoirleach 
paid tribute to Tipperary’s Senior and under 20 hurling champions noting the 
pride and unity these achievements brought to the county. He acknowledged 
the dedication of clubs, schools, coaches, families and volunteers whose 
commitment continues to sustain Gaelic Games throughout Tipperary.  
Cllr. Carroll outlined the successful Council delegation visit to Maryland, which 
strengthened links established through the Tipperary–Maryland Friendship 
Agreement. The visit included engagements with political, business, 
educational and agricultural representatives and explored opportunities in 
trade, research, education and the equine sector. He noted that the visit had 
already resulted in valuable business and educational connections and had the 
potential to deliver long-term benefits for the county.

The Cathaoirleach also highlighted several key challenges facing Tipperary, 
including the continued underfunding of regional and local roads, the need for 
housing and wastewater infrastructure in smaller settlements, declining water 
quality, and ongoing pressures on the agricultural sector. He emphasised the 
importance of supporting rural communities, protecting natural resources and 
ensuring the long-term sustainability of farming in the county. 
In relation to climate action and renewable energy, he welcomed progress 
made in the county but stressed the need for balanced development, adequate 
infrastructure and meaningful community engagement. He also expressed 
concern regarding proposals for water abstraction from the River Shannon and 
called for the protection of this important environmental, economic and 
tourism resource. Furthermore, he advocated for stronger local government 
powers and resources to enable councils to effectively meet the needs of their 
communities. 
Concluding his address, Cllr. Carroll expressed confidence in Tipperary’s future, 
citing the county’s resilience, strong community spirit and dedicated public 
representatives and staff. He thanked members for the honour of serving as 
Cathaoirleach, wished his successor every success in the role and reaffirmed 
his commitment to supporting the work of Tipperary County Council in the 
future.  
The Party Whips present at the meeting and Cllr. Michael Lowry on behalf of 
the Independent Group and members paid tribute to the outgoing 
Cathaoirleach on his successful term of office.   
Members expressed their appreciation to the outgoing Cathaoirleach, Cllr. John 
Carroll, for his dedicated service during the past year. They commended him 
for the manner in which he carried out his duties, noting his fairness, 
professionalism and commitment to representing the county. Members also 
highlighted his approachable nature and his ability to engage positively with 
councillors, staff and communities across Tipperary, wishing him every success 
in the future. 
Ms Sinéad Carr, Chief Executive, acknowledged the honour and privilege of 
serving as First Citizen of the County and recognised the significant 
commitment and hard work required of the role. She paid tribute to Cllr. John 
Carroll the way he carried out his duties as Cathaoirleach, noting that he had 
undertaken the role with professionalism, dedication and great pride. Ms Carr 
thanked him for his service to the Council and the people of Tipperary 
throughout the year and stated that he should be very proud of all that he had 
achieved during his term of office. 
 
The outgoing Cathaoirleach then sought nominations for the position of 
Cathaoirleach.

Brennan, and agreed,

Council for the year 2026-2027”.   
In proposing her Labour Party colleague, Cllr. Morgan Walsh stated that it was 
a proud day for the Labour Party and a significant occasion for Tipperary 
County Council. She paid tribute to Cllr. Fiona Bonfield’s late father, 
acknowledging the role he had played in supporting and encouraging her

Address of 
Incoming 
Cathaoirleach 
 
In her inaugural address following her election as Cathaoirleach of Tipperary 
County Council, Cllr. Fiona Bonfield expressed her gratitude to her fellow 
councillors for the trust and confidence they had placed in her. She described 
her appointment as a great honour and a historic milestone, noting that she 
was proud to serve as a Labour Party Cathaoirleach and to represent all 
communities across the county. She emphasised her commitment to 
inclusivity, equality and community engagement, acknowledging the 
importance of ensuring that local government reflects the diverse needs and 
aspirations of the people it serves. She also paid tribute to the outgoing 
Cathaoirleach, Cllr. John Carroll, thanking him for his dedication and service 
throughout his term of office. 
Cllr. Bonfield outlined her vision and priorities for the year ahead, identifying 
affordable housing, tackling vacancy and dereliction, and fostering more 
connected and thriving communities as the key areas she wished to champion 
during her term. She highlighted the ongoing housing challenges facing 
families and individuals across the county and stressed the importance of 
increasing the supply of affordable homes, maximising available funding 
opportunities and strengthening collaboration between local authorities, 
housing bodies and community organisations. She stated that young people 
and families should have the opportunity to live and prosper in their own 
communities and committed to advocating for practical housing solutions 
throughout the county. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
The Cathaoirleach also emphasised the need to address vacancy and 
dereliction in towns and villages across Tipperary. She noted that many 
properties remain vacant while housing demand continues to increase and 
businesses seek opportunities to grow. She called for continued support for 
initiatives that bring vacant and derelict buildings back into productive use, 
revitalise town centres, preserve local heritage and encourage investment and 
economic activity. She described the regeneration of such properties as an 
important means of strengthening communities and restoring confidence in 
local areas. 
Addressing community development, Cllr. Bonfield said that strong and 
resilient communities are built through investment, partnership and 
participation. She highlighted the importance of supporting community groups, 
improving infrastructure, investing in recreational and cultural amenities and 
ensuring that both rural and urban communities have opportunities to grow 
and prosper. She stated that connectivity extends beyond physical 
infrastructure and includes ensuring that older people are supported, young 
people are provided with opportunities to succeed and that all citizens feel 
valued, included and connected within their communities. 
Cllr. Bonfield also outlined her commitment to sustainable development and 
balanced economic growth. She spoke positively about Tipperary’s many 
strengths, including its strong agricultural sector, thriving business community, 
rich heritage, cultural assets and outstanding natural environment. She 
highlighted the county’s tourism potential, referencing landmarks such as the
