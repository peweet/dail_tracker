4. To consider Monaghan Co Council Draft Heritage Strategy 2026 - 2031 
Ms. K. Ward, Heritage Officer, delivered a presentation to members on the Draft 
Heritage Strategy 2026–2031. She outlined that the Strategy provides a clear roadmap 
for the protection, management, and sustainable use of heritage across the county over 
the next five years. It aligns local priorities with national policy, ensuring a proactive and 
strategic approach to heritage planning. 
 
Ms. Ward advised that the Strategy has been developed through extensive consultation, 
including engagement with the Heritage Forum, the hosting of workshops and public 
information days, and the undertaking of public consultation exercises. She noted that 
the final document incorporates expert contributions and reflects community priorities. 
 
Members were reminded that heritage extends beyond built structures and monuments 
to include landscapes, biodiversity, traditions, language, and skills. The county currently 
has approximately 700 protected structures, 1,500 recorded monuments, as well as 
significant habitats, landscapes, and conservation areas. 
 
The overarching aim of the Strategy is to safeguard and celebrate heritage while also 
using it to support wellbeing, reinforce identity, and promote sustainable development. 
The Strategy is underpinned by five key strategic themes: 
1. Protect and Advocate – Leading the conservation and sustainable management of 
heritage assets and embedding heritage considerations in planning, policy, and 
decision-making. 
2. Grow Knowledge – Enhancing research, data collection, and interpretation to 
support evidence-based decision-making and improve access to heritage knowledge. 
3. Empower Communities – Promoting inclusive engagement with heritage through 
education, events, citizen science, and support for local initiatives. 
4. Nurture Partnerships – Strengthening collaboration at local, national, and cross-
border level to support heritage protection, promotion, and innovation. 
5. Build Climate Resilience – Integrating nature-based and sustainable approaches to 
address climate impacts and protect biodiversity. 

3 
 
 
Ms. Ward highlighted that the Strategy would act as a key driver for regeneration, 
tourism, community development, wellbeing, and climate action in the county over the 
coming five years. 
 
On the proposal of Cllr P. Conlon, seconded by Cllr S. Conlon, it was agreed that the Draft 
Heritage Strategy 2026-2031, as presented, be approved.  
 
The members acknowledged the volume of work involved in the development of the 
Heritage Strategy 2026-2031 and conveyed their gratitude to Ms. Ward and all involved 
for their input.

6. To approve nominations for appointments to the Disability Steering Committee  
Ms. N. Payne, Senior Executive Officer, Community & Enterprise, joined the meeting to 
provide members with an overview of the role and membership of the Disability Steering 
Committee. She stated that role of Monaghan County Council’s Disability Steering 
Committee will be to ensure that services across the county are accessible to people 
with a disability. This will include the development, implementation and monitoring of a 
5-year Disability Strategy. The committee will also strive to ensure the needs of people 
with a disability are embedded in the policies, plans and strategies of Monaghan County 
Council and its wider partner agencies. The Disability Steering Committee will be 
established as a subcommittee of the Local Community Development Committee (LCDC) 
and will report to them on a quarterly basis.  
 
Ms. Payne stated that the committee will ensure that the services of Monaghan County 
Council and its key partners are accessible to people with a disability. No physical or 
discriminatory barriers should prevent them from engaging fully as users of all services. 
Ms. Payne concluded by providing members with an overview of the proposed 
membership of the Disability Steering Committee which includes 3 elected members (1 
elected member from each municipal district).

9. To receive a presentation on 'Place Branding' at the May Co Co Meeting 
Ms. C. O’Hare, Director of Services welcomed Ms. G. Morgan, Innovation and Marketing 
Strategy Lead, to the meeting to provide members with a presentation on ‘Place 
Branding’. Ms. O’Hare advised the members that the requirement for ‘Place Branding’ 
had been identified during the development of the Tourism Strategy 2023-2028 and the 
Council committed to delivering this within the Corporate Plan 2024-2029. Ms. O’Hare 
noted that the ‘Place Branding’ project is not corporate rebranding, however, the work 
that has been carried out to date has looked at how both brands will co-exist.  
  
Ms. Morgan provided Members with a brief overview of the work undertaken to date in 
respect of the Place Branding Project. The following key points were noted: 
- 
The primary objective of the project is to develop an overarching proposition and 
place brand that will showcase County Monaghan both locally and internationally as 
an attractive location to live, work, visit and invest/do business. 
- 
It was acknowledged that, while there are currently a number of strong marketing 
initiatives promoting tourism and business within the County, these efforts are 
fragmented and lack a cohesive ‘Master Brand’. 

6 
 
- 
The proposed Place Brand is designed to be simple and clean, utilising upper-case 
lettering to convey confidence and pride. A soft arch feature has been incorporated 
to symbolise opportunity and optimism within County Monaghan. 
- 
Members were advised that the Place Brand will complement, and not replace, the 
traditional Monaghan County Council coat of arms. 
- 
The typeface used in the branding has been specifically designed for County 
Monaghan and draws inspiration from the elliptical forms and strokes of Gregg 
Shorthand, a system developed in 1888 by Monaghan native John Robert Gregg. 
- 
A shorthand version of the logotype, “MN”, will be used alongside both the Place 
Brand and the traditional coat of arms in appropriate applications. 
The members thanked Ms. Morgan for her informative presentation and commended her 
and all involved for their input on the ‘Place Branding’ project.

26. 
With regard to the water quality in Lough Muckno and ongoing works to improve the quality of 
Muckno: 
a) What is the latest update on this work? 
b) What plans have Monaghan County Council in place for testing the water quality to ensure 
swimming etc. may occur? 
Reply:  
a. Lough Muckno has been included as a Priority Area for Action in Irelands Water Action Plan (2022-
2027).  Work is underway at a national and regional level to develop water action plans for all sectors 
and agencies involved in water framework directive implementation.  In respect of work being carried 
out by Environmental Services staff in the Muckno catchment area, catchment characterisation and 
inspections are ongoing in the County Water and the Gentle Owens River Catchment areas. Our work 
to date on the Lough Muckno project has highlighted these areas as the catchment areas contributing 
the most significant pollutant loadings to Lough Muckno.  Farm inspections are also taking place in the 
Fane 10 catchment upstream of Lough Muckno. Farm inspection work now makes up a significant 
portion of our water quality work in Environmental Services as “Pressures from Agriculture (Farmyards) 
- slurry/soiled water collection and storage” and “Pressures from Agriculture (Farmland) - slurry and 
fertiliser spreading “ are two National Enforcement Priority areas as directed by the EPA on which Local 
Authorities have to allocate significant time and resource to.   
 
Monaghan County Council is also an associate partner on the PEACE 5.3 plus Project titled “Protecting 
our Shared Waters”. The role of Monaghan County Council as Associate Partner will be to provide local 
expertise on the Fane catchment and to support the selection of a demonstration farm which can be 
used for knowledge exchange purposes. Monaghan County Council is due to meet shortly with the 
Project Lead Partners and will continue to provide local expertise and support to the project throughout 
2026 and beyond. 
 
Since January, Environmental Services in collaboration with other local groups and cross border bodies 
have been exploring funding opportunities to develop a cross-border collaborative work plan for the 
inputting river catchments which are contributing the most significant loads of pollutant to Lough 
Muckno. Confirmation on whether these funding applications have been successful has yet to be issued 
by the awarding bodies.  
 
b. In June Environmental Services will be commencing sampling under the 2026 Bathing Waters 
Sampling program. Although there are no designated Bathing Waters in the county and Monaghan 
County Council has no obligation to conduct the sampling of any bathing waters, sampling of a 
number of non-designated bathing waters will be taking place over the summer months during the 
bathing season. In relation to Lough Muckno, Monaghan County Council will be carrying out bathing 
water sampling at the following locations in the period from June to September: 

19 
 
• 
Lough Muckno Bathing area 
• 
   Lough Muckno Water Sports Area  
• 
   Lough Muckno Black Island Point 
These sites listed above shall be assessed for compliance with the water quality standards specified in 
the 2008 Bathing Water Regulations, using the two microbiological parameters, Intestinal enterococci, 
and Escherichia coli (e-coli). Samples will be taken and analysed by our contracted laboratory. It takes 
three to four days for the results of sampling to be completed and returned by the lab. The planned 
monitoring is aimed at providing an overall assessment of bathing water quality and there is no 
absolute guarantee that on the day that an individual chooses to bathe at one of these locations that 
there may not be an issue with water quality. Water quality can vary significantly during the day, and 
from day to day, due e.g. to the influences of rain or sunshine and the number of bathers using the 
water at any time. Bathing water quality is however at its most vulnerable after heavy rainfall, or in 
stormy conditions, which may give rise to higher bacterial counts than normal. In the event of the 
detection of a Poor-Quality Result the council will: 
• 
Erect a notice at the site advising not to swim. 
• 
Arrange for a further sample to be taken. 
In recent years the bathing water quality at the Muckno bathing area location has consistently been 
below standard and Environmental Services is of the opinion is that this can be attributed to the fact 
that this is a very popular location for the feeding of ducks and it is the high concentration of ducks in 
this area that is contributing to the poor bathing water quality. Environmental services would advise 
that the public refrain from swimming at this particular location and have erected an advisory notice 
on site to this effect at this location. 
 
However, it should be noted that the sampling results for the other sampling locations at the Water 
Sports Area and at Black Island on Lough Muckno have consistently indicated that the bathing water 
quality at these locations is good.  
 
In 2026 all monitoring results at the designated locations will be available to view on the national 
bathing waters website www.beaches.ie

Retirement – Ms. P. Monahan, Director of Services  
Cllr PJ O’Hanlon led the tributes to Ms. P. Monahan, Director of Services as she attended her last 
meeting of Monaghan County Council prior to her retirement. Cllr O’Hanlon paid tribute to the 
diligent and professional way in which Ms. Monahan carried out her role. He conveyed his gratitude 
to Ms. Monahan for her voluntary contributions to the community in Monaghan and her 
commitment to helping others. Cllr O’Hanlon wished Ms. Monahan a long and healthy retirement.  
 
Mr. R. Burns, Chief Executive, outlined Ms. Monahan’s distinguished service with Monaghan County 
Council, which commenced in September 2014 in the Finance Section. He noted that in February 
2017, Ms. Monahan was appointed to the position of Director of Services with responsibility for 
Infrastructure and Operations, and that in 2019 her role changed to Director of Services for 
Transport, Community and Rural Development. The Chief Executive referred to Ms. Monahan’s term 
as Interim Chief Executive from September 2022 to September 2023 and conveyed his sincere 
gratitude for the guidance and support she provided both in the months prior to his appointment 
and subsequently. Mr. Burns paid tribute to Ms. Monahan’s significant contribution to the 
development of the Climate Action Plan 2024–2029 in her role as Director of Services for 
Environment and Climate Action from September 2023. He also acknowledged her input into the 
Tourism Strategy 2023–2028 and her work on the Ulster Canal Marina, Clones, in her current role as 
Director of Tourism, Rural and Community Development. The Chief Executive commended Ms. 
Monahan’s diligent and meticulous work style and referred to the wide range of responsibilities she 
discharged during her tenure, including Transport, Climate Action, Procurement, Community and 
Tourism, as well as her role within Monaghan Municipal District. He noted that Ms. Monahan 
possesses a wealth of organisational knowledge and described her as consistently helpful and 
courteous in all her dealings. The Chief Executive concluded by thanking Ms. Monahan for her 
dedicated service and congratulated her on her retirement, wishing her every happiness and success 
in the future. 
 
The members joined in the tributes to Ms. P. Monahan and wished her well in her retirement.  
 
Ms. Monahan commenced by thanking all contributors for their kind words and well wishes. She 
stated that it had been her privilege to serve the organisation and reflected on her experience of 
working in the public sector, having joined from a background in private sector accounting. She 
noted that the transition had been both productive and fulfilling, while at times presenting its own 
challenges. She outlined that for the majority of her time with Monaghan County Council she had 
served in the role of Director/Acting Director, and in those roles had the support of a wide range of 
colleagues across the various sections that came within her remit over the years. Ms. Monahan 
extended her sincere thanks to her current teams in Monaghan Municipal District, Tourism Rural & 
Community Development, describing them as a pleasure to work with. She also expressed her 
appreciation to the Chief Executive, Mr. Robert Burns, and to her colleagues on the Management 
Team, noting the strong sense of collaboration that had developed over time. She further 
acknowledged the support services, including ICT, Finance, Human Resources and Corporate 
Services, describing them as consistently helpful and supportive, and as a vital engine that keeps the 
organisation functioning. She thanked the elected members for their engagement and noted that 
she had enjoyed a productive and positive working relationship with them. She expressed her hope 
that she and her teams had supported the members in their work on behalf of their constituents, 
acknowledging the increasing challenges associated with that role. Ms. Monahan also welcomed the 
increased participation of women within the Council, noting that when she was appointed Director 
of Services she was the first woman to hold such a position in almost 120 years of the organisation’s 
history. She emphasised the importance of full representation at all levels within the organisation 
and in the Council Chamber, and expressed her hope that more women would continue to come 
forward and have their contributions recognised. 

23 
 
 
In conclusion, Ms. Monahan wished the Elected Members, Chief Executive, Management Team and 
staff of Monaghan County Council every success for the future and stated that it had been a pleasure 
to have been part of the organisation.
