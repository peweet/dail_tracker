Minutes of the 2026 Annual General Meeting of Clare County Council
Location:  Held in the Council Chamber, Áras Contae an Chláir, New Road, Ennis, Co. Clare and via MS Teams
On:  	Friday 26th June 2026 
Time:  	3pm
Present
Cllrs. P. Daly, M. Howard, P. Murphy, A. Baker Bashua, T. Guilfoyle, C. Colleran Molloy, T. O’Callaghan, P. Hayes, A. O’Callaghan, P. Burke, T. O’Brien, C. Ryan, D. Griffin, R. Hartigan, J. Crowe, P. O’Gorman, M. Begley, J. Ryan, B. Slattery, J. Garrihy, J. Killeen, D. Gould, R. McInerney, I. Lynch, G. Keating, M. Shannon.
Officials
Mr. Gordon Daly, Chief Executive.
Mr. Alan Farrell, Deputy Chief Executive & Director of Housing Delivery, Infrastructure Provision & Land Activation.
Ms. Mary McMahon, A/Meetings Administrator.
Ms. Aoife Coote, Assistant Staff Officer, Corporate Services & Governance.
Ms. Ann Reynolds, Director of People & Culture, Corporate Services & Governance, ICT & Digital Services.
Dr. Carmel Kirby, Director of Transportation, Climate Action, Environmental & Emergency Services.
Ms. Noeleen Fitzgerald, Director of Finance, Investment & Enterprise.
Ms. Siobhán McNulty, Director of Tourism, Recreation, Libraries & Cultural Services.
Mr. Jason Murphy, Director of Planning, Economic, Rural & Community Development.
Present via MS Teams:
Cllrs. T. Mulcahy, S. Talty
Presiding
The outgoing Cathaoirleach, Cllr. P. Murphy
reflected on a busy and rewarding year in office and thanked the elected members for their support and the opportunity to serve in the role. He acknowledged the cooperation and respect shown by members throughout the year, which contributed to the effective conduct of Council meetings.
Marking Your Council Day, he highlighted the important work carried out by Clare County Council and recognised the commitment and professionalism of staff in delivering essential services and supporting communities across the county.
The Cathaoirleach spoke of the privilege of attending events throughout Clare, meeting volunteers and community groups, and welcomed the designation of Clare as the European Capital of Volunteering 2027, describing it as a significant opportunity to promote the county.
He also reflected on overseas engagements, including visits to Milwaukee and New York to meet members of the Clare diaspora during Irish cultural and St. Patrick’s Day events.
The Cathaoirleach acknowledged a number of sporting achievements celebrated during the year, including successes by local clubs and athletes, and congratulated the Clare Under-20 Hurling team on their recent Munster and All-Ireland championship victories.
Reference was made to notable events in the county, including the opening of the Springfield Flood Relief Scheme and a visit by the Chinese Ambassador. The Cathaoirleach also highlighted the  charity cycle he organised, which raised almost €5,000 for the Samaritans, and thanked all participants and supporters, particularly Clare Civil Defence.
He acknowledged recent bereavements among staff and elected members and extended his sympathies to those affected. In concluding, he thanked the Leas-Cathaoirleach, the Chief Executive, directors, staff, and officials for their support throughout the year, with special recognition given to John Corry, Senior Executive Officer, for his dedication and service.
The elected members paid tributes to the outgoing Cathaoirleach, Cllr. P. Murphy and complimented the manner which he carried out his duties and co-ordinated the meetings during the year.
G. Daly, Chief Executive, acknowledged the outgoing Cathaoirleach, Cllr. P. Murphy and commended him for his outstanding service and representation of County Clare throughout his term. He noted that the Cathaoirleach had been an excellent ambassador for the county, both nationally and internationally and reflected on a successful year that brought him from Loop Head to Whitegate and further afield to locations such as Carnegie Hall and Barcelona.
On behalf of the Management and staff of Clare County Council, Mr. Daly wished Cllr. Murphy every success for the future. He then invited A. Coote, Assistant Staff Officer, Corporate Services and Governance, to present Cllr. Murphy with a gift from the staff of a commemorative photobook capturing highlights of his year as Cathaoirleach.
Item 1: Election of Cathaoirleach of the Council for the coming year.
The outgoing Cathaoirleach called for nominations for the post of Cathaoirleach for the forthcoming year.
Cllr. T. O’Callaghan proposed Cllr. J. Killeen for the position of Cathaoirleach of Clare County Council for the year 2026-2027, this proposal was seconded by Cllr. P. Daly and agreed by all present.
As there were no other nominations, Cllr. J. Killeen was declared elected as Cathaoirleach of Clare County Council for the coming year.
The Cathaoirleach, Cllr. J. Killeen thanked the Members for nominating him to the position of Cathaoirleach and stated that he was humbled and privileged to assume the role. He acknowledged the long tradition of service within Clare County Council and paid tribute to his predecessor, Cllr. Paul Murphy, for his leadership and representation of the County during his term of office.
Cllr. Killeen welcomed the power-sharing agreement within the Council Chamber, noting that it would provide all groups with an opportunity to play a meaningful and cooperative role during the Council term. He emphasised the importance of working collectively for the benefit of the people of Clare while remaining mindful of the Council’s commitment to its heritage.
The Cathaoirleach outlined a number of priorities for his term of office. He highlighted the importance of supporting and developing tourism as a key driver of employment and economic activity throughout the County. He commended the work of the Council’s Tourism Directorate and welcomed initiatives such as the Burren Explorer Bus, which demonstrates cooperation between a range of transport and tourism stakeholders. He also noted the significance of tourism attractions across the County, including the Cliffs of Moher, Vandeleur Walled Garden, Loop Head and Inis Cealtra, as well as major sporting events taking place in Doonbeg and Lahinch.
Housing was identified as a key priority. Cllr. Killeen referred to the recent variation to the County Development Plan, which provided opportunities for lands to be made available for development, and stressed the need to facilitate increased housing supply throughout the County. He welcomed plans to unlock lands for residential development in Ennis through the Housing Infrastructure Fund and highlighted the importance of collaboration between Clare County Council, Uisce Éireann, ESB Networks and other State agencies. He also welcomed the forthcoming Rural Housing Guidelines and the opportunities they may provide to support rural housing development.
The Cathaoirleach emphasised the need for increased funding for roads infrastructure, noting that additional resources are required to maintain and improve the road network and to progress projects within each Municipal District. He acknowledged the work of road crews throughout the County.
Cllr. Killeen stated that balanced regional development would remain a priority and highlighted the importance of supporting Shannon Airport, the Shannon Estuary and the continued rollout of broadband infrastructure.
He also stressed the importance of supporting educational institutions throughout the County, including primary, secondary and third-level education providers. He acknowledged the valuable contribution of programmes supporting those affected by educational disadvantage and unemployment, including the SICAP Scheme and Lifelong Learning initiatives delivered through Limerick and Clare Education and Training Board.
The Cathaoirleach paid tribute to the many community and voluntary organisations operating throughout Clare, including community groups, the Clare Volunteer Centre, the Clare Local Community Safety Partnership, the Clare Leader Forum, the Clare Traveller Community Development Project, suicide prevention initiatives, Clare Youth Service and the Local Authority Integration Team.
He further highlighted the need to progress the provision of a hospital in Ennis, improve healthcare services for those living a distance from University Hospital Limerick, and continue to advocate for improved childcare provision and supports for older people.
The Cathaoirleach expressed his appreciation to the Chief Executive and staff of Clare County Council and looked forward to working with them during his term of office. He acknowledged the Council’s success in continuing to deliver quality services to communities throughout County Clare despite the challenges faced.
In concluding, Cllr. Killeen thanked his family, relatives and supporters for their encouragement and support. He expressed his commitment to working with all Members to ensure cooperation and progress for the benefit of all those living in County Clare.
The members present congratulated Cathaoirleach, Cllr. Joe Killeen on his appointment as Cathaoirleach of Clare County Council, wished him well and committed their support during his term.
The elected members commended the power sharing arrangement that has been in place for the benefit of the county.
Item 2: Election of Leas Cathaoirleach of Council for coming year.
Leas-Cathaoirleach, Cllr. C. Colleran Molloy, paid tribute to the outgoing Cathaoirleach, Cllr. P. Murphy and thanked him for his dedication and leadership throughout the year. She noted the positive working relationship they had enjoyed during their respective terms and expressed her hope that she had provided effective support to him in his role. Cllr. Colleran Molloy further acknowledged the enthusiasm, commitment and energy that Cllr. Murphy brought to the position of Cathaoirleach, which had contributed significantly to a successful year.
The Cathaoirleach, Cllr. J. Killeen called for nominations for the position of Leas Cathaoirleach.
Cllr. P. Burke proposed Cllr. T. Mulcahy for the position of Leas Cathaoirleach and this was seconded by Cllr. B. Slattery and agreed by all present.
As there were no other nominations for the post the Cathaoirleach declared Cllr. T. Mulcahy elected as Leas Cathaoirleach for the coming year.
Cllr. J. Crowe accepted the Chain of Office on behalf of the newly elected Leas-Cathaoirleach, Cllr. T. Mulcahy, who was unable to attend the meeting. Cllr. Crowe addressed the meeting and delivered a speech on behalf of Cllr. Mulcahy, in which he thanked Cllr. P. Burke and Cllr. B. Slattery for proposing and seconding his nomination as Leas-Cathaoirleach.
Cllr. Mulcahy expressed his commitment to supporting the Cathaoirleach in the discharge of his duties throughout the coming year. He also acknowledged the longstanding power sharing agreement within Clare County Council, which has endured for more than 22 years. Reflecting on his involvement from the early stages of this collaborative approach, he stated that he was delighted to once again work alongside his fellow Councillors and the Executive in serving the people of County Clare. He reaffirmed his commitment to working collectively to ensure that Clare continues to be one of the best places in Ireland in which to live and work.
G. Daly, Chief Executive congratulated Cathaoirleach, Cllr. J. Killeen and Leas Cathaoirleach, Cllr. T. Mulcahy and assured them of his full support and commitment for the year ahead as well as the support of the Council staff.
Item 3: Schedule of Ordinary Meetings for next twelve months.
The Schedule of Meetings was circulated with the agenda and was proposed by Cllr. M. Howard, seconded by Cllr. A. O’Callaghan and agreed by the members present.
Item 4: Approval for payment of the Cathaoirleach and Leas Cathaoirleach allowances for the forthcoming year.
Approval for payment of the Cathaoirleach and Leas Cathaoirleach allowances for the forthcoming year was proposed by Cllr. P. Hayes, seconded by Cllr. P. Daly and agreed by the members present.
Conclusion
The meeting then concluded.
