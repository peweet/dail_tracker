The Cathaoirleach read out the Emergency Notice of Motion. 
Proposed by Cllr. F. Phelan, 
Seconded by Cllr. F.Browne 
 
“That Carlow County Council welcomes and supports the measures announced by 
Government to assist households, farmers, and businesses with the rising cost of 
fuel and energy. 
 
In particular, the Council supports: 
 
• The further reduction in excise duty on diesel by 10 cent per litre (VAT 
inclusive), bringing the total reduction to 32 cent per litre; 
 
• The further reduction in excise duty on petrol by 10 cent per litre (VAT 
inclusive), bringing the total reduction to 27 cent per litre; 
 
• The inclusion of NORA levy adjustments within these overall reductions; 
 
• The deferral of the planned Carbon Tax increase, due on 1 May, until the 
time of the Budget, particularly in respect of Green Diesel and non-propellant 
fuels such as home heating oil, natural gas, and solid fuels. 
 
The Council further notes that these excise reductions will take effect from 
midnight on Wednesday, 15 April 2026, and will remain in place until 31 July 2026. 
 
Carlow County Council recognises the importance of these measures in easing 
cost-of-living pressures, particularly for rural communities, commuters, the 
agricultural and haulage sector.”

Alteration to Proposed Amendment no. 2(a) 
Requests the rezoning of 4ha of land from ‘Strategic Reserve’ to 
‘New Residential’ with an equivalent rezoning of ‘New Residential’ 
to ‘Strategic Reserve’ at the northern end of the landholding. 
 
Alteration to Proposed Amendment no. 2(c) 
Requests rezoning of 3.4ha on the landholding at Pollerton Big 
from ‘Open Space and Amenity’ to ‘New Residential’. 
 
Also seeks corresponding change to Policy CW. H1 to include 
requirement for 3.4ha of public open space to inform future layout, 
design, and phased development of the landholding. 
 
 
 
 

 
 
Alteration to Proposed Amendment no. 2(a) 
Not recommended.  The land use zonings at this location the 
subject of Proposed Amendment no. 2(a), have been informed by 
the Study Area and Route Corridor Options identified in the Phase 1 
and Phase 2 stages for the Carlow Southern Relief Road (CSSR) 
Project, a long-term strategic transport project designed to support 
the future growth and sustainability of Carlow Town and the wider 
region, the delivery of which has been identified as a strategic 
project in the National Development Plan 2021-2030. Carlow 
County Council, in partnership with Laois County Council and 
Department of Transport (DoT), are progressing the Carlow 
Southern Relief Road (CSRR) project. Taking account of the 
foregoing, including the strategic requirement to safeguard the 
Study Area and Route Corridor Options for the CSSR Project, it is 
considered that the alteration sought to Proposed Amendment no. 
2(a) would not be appropriate.  
 
Alteration to Proposed Amendment no. 2(c) 
Not recommended.  This area of dedicated zoned public open 
space has been identified for a number of reasons:  
▪ To ensure strategic designated open space and amenity 
zoned land is specifically provided for as part of the statutory 
plan making process. 
▪ To ensure the preservation of built and natural features at this 
location, including protected structures (RPS Ref.CW442 
and CW443), the layout of the historic demesne landscape, 
the location of historic access routes to the landholding, 
boundary features, etc. 
▪ To protect, provide for and enhance open space, amenity and 
recreational facilities within Carlow Environs, which are 
accessible beyond the immediate environs of the proposed 
‘New Residential’ zoned lands.  
▪ To provide an important visual break in the urban built 
environment at this location within Carlow Environs, will 
support the retention and maintenance of existing built and 
natural heritage features, and will support the provision of an 
area of strategic green space / green infrastructure. 
▪ The provision of this public open space in conjunction with 
open space requirements as detailed in the development 

 
 
management standards (section 16.8.7) as contained in the 
Carlow County Development Plan 2022-2028 will seek to 
ensure a high-quality residential environment with the 
dedicated allocation of lands to accommodate open space 
and amenity uses.

6.0 Notices of Motion/Fógraí Rúin  
6.1 Standing in the name of Cllr. Daniel Pender 
Carlow County Council notes that the State’s gambling laws were written 
before the rise of modern betting prediction markets such as Polymarket.  
These platforms allow people to place wagers on a wide range of speculative 
events, from whether the Iranian Ayatollah will remain in power by the end of 
the month to whether Jef Bezos will attend the Super Bowl.  Such markets are 
dynamic, constantly shifting, and fundamentally different from the types of bets 
traditionally offered by bookmakers.  
 

 
 
In light of this, Carlow County Council is calling for urgent reform of gambling 
legislation to outlaw these grotesque and immoral markets, which risk serving 
as a gateway addition. 
 
Carlow County Council will write to the Department of Justice to request that 
these reforms be brought forward.    
  
Proposed by Cllr. D. Pender, 
Seconded by Cllr. J. Pender, 
And agreed following a show of hands 
 
6.2  Standing in the name of Cllrs. J. Cassin, W. Paton, A. Wallace, J. 
Deane, W. Quinn, F. Phelan and M. Doran 
Carlow County Council calls on Bus Éireann to retain Expressway Route 4 
(Dublin Airport-Waterford) and not proceed with the planned withdrawal from 
24 May 2026, recognising the significant importance of this service to 
passengers in Carlow and surrounding areas 
 
• 
Carlow County Council notes the announcement that Expressway Route 
4, along with parts of Routes 40 and 52, is due to be withdrawn from 24 
May 2026; 
• 
Recognises that Route 4 provides a vital connection between Carlow, 
Dublin Heuston, Busáras and Dublin Airport; 
• 
Further notes that the service currently operates 12 times daily and is 
used by commuters, students, hospital users, workers and airport 
passengers; 
• 
Carlow County Council believes the withdrawal will have a serious 
negative impact on regional connectivity and accessibility; 
• 
Calls on Bus Éireann and the National Transport Authority to urgently 
engage with affected communities and representatives to keep the 
service in operation; 
• 
Requests that alternative measures, supports or service adjustments be 
explored before any final decision is implemented;  
• 
Examine, as a matter of urgency, the introduction of a Public Service 
Obligation (PSO) on this route to ensure the continued provision of a 
reliable and accessible service between Waterford and Dublin via 
Carlow.  
 
Proposed by Cllr. J. Cassin,  
Seconded by Cllr. F. Phelan, 
And agreed following a show of hands
