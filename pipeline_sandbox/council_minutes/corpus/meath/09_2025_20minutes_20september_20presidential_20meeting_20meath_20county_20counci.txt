An Cathaoirleach, Councillor Wayne Harding, noted that in accordance with Section 16 of the Presidential Election 
Act, 1993, a council of a county or a corporation of a county borough may, by resolution, resolve to nominate one 
named person to be a candidate at a presidential election.  He further noted that 17 candidates had requested the 
support of Meath County Council and had verified their eligibility for nomination for the Election to the Office of 
the President of Ireland.  He noted that each candidate would be allocated a maximum of 15 minutes, comprised of 
5 minutes presentation/speaking time and 10 minutes for questions and answers from the Elected Members. 
 
He further noted that in accordance with the Council’s standing orders, all acts of the Council which are reserved 
functions or questions duly coming or arising before a meeting of the Council shall be determined:  
(a) by a majority of the Councillors present and voting, or  
(b) where there is an equality of votes, by a second or casting vote of the person chairing the meeting, should they 
choose to exercise such a vote. 
 
 

 
 
 
 
 
 
Miontuairiscí / Meeting Minutes 
 
Councillor David Gilroy noted that several other Local Authorities had recently completed the process but 
ultimately chose not to put forward a candidate for consideration. 
 
Councillor Gerry O’Connor proposed to suspend standing orders, to consider a motion not to propose any 
candidate for nomination for the Election to the Office of the President of Ireland.  This was seconded by Councillor 
Joe Fox. 
 
There were interventions from Councillors Joe Fox, Joe Bonner, Nick Killian, Alan Lawes, Brian Fitzgerald, Carol 
Lennon and Sharon Tolan.  The majority of Elected Members expressed the view that it was unfair to deny 
candidates the opportunity to present their cases.  Conversely, some members contended that those seeking the 
Council's support had not adequately demonstrated their commitment to public service. 
 
The Meetings Administrator noted that in accordance with the provisions of the Standing Orders Governing the 
Procedures of the Council, the suspension of standing orders requires the approval of a minimum of two-thirds of 
the Members present.
