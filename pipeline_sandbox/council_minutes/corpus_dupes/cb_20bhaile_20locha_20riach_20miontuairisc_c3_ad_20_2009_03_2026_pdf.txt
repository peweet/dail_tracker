ROAD SAFETY / SPEED ENFORCEMENT MEASURES
Garda Superintendent Oliver Baker, An Garda Siochåna.
LMD2470
Cllr. Killilea welcomed Garda Superintendent Oliver Baker to the meeting.
Garda Supt Baker thanked the Members for the invitation and advised the meeting that he
holds responsibility for road traffic legislation and road safety across the Loughrea
operational area, including Loughrea, Portumna, Gort, Kilrickle, Kinvara, Eyrecourt, Killimor,
and Woodford. Supt. Baker provided an overview of road safety statistics for the 12-month
period from February 2025 to February 2026. During this period, the region recorded:
4 fatalities from road traffic collisions (rtc's)
• 5 collisions resulting in serious injuries
385 material damage incidents (recorded primarily for insurance purposes)
Supt. Baker noted that while the number of fatal incidents remains relatively low, any fatal
accident was one too many, and there were ongoing efforts to bring about positive
improvements in driver's behaviour.
Supt. Baker also presented other data, including the number of fines issued for speeding,
mobile phone use while driving, failure to wear seat belts, driving under the influence and
Other Fixed Charge Notices (FCNs). Garda Supt Baker explained that the establishment of
checkpoints is evidence-based, guided by local knowledge, data, and risk patterns, and
welcomed input from the Members in highlighting their local concerns in this regard. He
emphasised that the team's approach is proactive, focusing on prevention rather than
detection alone and also highlighted the educational dimension of their work, noting that
the team engages with local schools to deliver road safety talks. In summary, Supt. Baker
stated that there were over 1,400 proactive checkpoints for enforcement representing 4
interventions per day, with nearly 2,200 road users randomly tested for drink or drugs, and
1,500 tickets for various offences issued last year.
Cllr. Killilea thanked Supt. Baker for the overview of enforcement activity and complemented
all emergency services for the exceptional job that they do. Cllr. Killilea welcomed the
emphasis on education and the need to focus on road safety and changing driver behaviour

as the way forward.
Cllr. Kelly stated that it was useful to have sight of the road safety figures and stressed that
the key issue is driver behaviour. He requested clarification on whether public
representatives may request the installation of speed cameras in specific areas and, if so,
what the formal process entails. He also queried whether local authority funding has any
role in supporting fixed camera infrastructure.
Cllr. Maher commented that financial penalties were, in his view, the most effective
deterrent to speeding and stated that GoSafe vans deliver strongest results. He stated he
would welcome further updates on road safety in the district, noting his previous experience
on the Joint Policing Committee and also expressed interest in understanding how the
proposed reduction in speed limits from 50 kph to 30 kph may affect Garda resources and
manpower requirements.
Garda Supt Baker advised that areas of concern should be notified to An Garda Siochåna.
Any applications for GoSafe vans within the district should be referred directly to An Garda
Siochåna, who will then make the formal application to OSCAM. Once an area is
nominated, a covert speed survey is undertaken, as he noted that perception of high-speed
areas is often not the reality, and therefore camera installations must be evidence based,
and then risk assessed to ensure viability. Where there are problem areas which cannot be
addressed through fixed cameras, then other measures such as local checkpoints can be
initiated.
Cllr. Regan commended An Garda Siochåna for their ongoing work and sought Garda Supt
Baker's views on the use of speed ramps at village entrances, stating that he considered
them an effective deterrent. Supt Baker responded that he supports any interventions that
enhance road safety and noted that engineering solutions—such as speed indicator signs,
road narrowing, ramps —need to be looked at in conjunction with the whole suite of
solutions for a problem area and discussed with the Roads Policing Unit.
Cllr. Kinane asked for the An Garda Siochåna perspective on the forthcoming speed limit
reductions and whether there would be sufficient resources to enforce the new legislation,
particularly on regional routes where speed limits were being reduced. She also expressed
the view that additional ramps and traffic calming measures should be introduced and
queried who she should contact to report problem locations. Supt Baker advised that An
Garda Siochåna will continue to use all available resources to monitor driver behaviour and
noted that monitoring 30 kph speeds is very challenging in many settings, with checkpoints
being the most effective enforcement approach. Supt. Baker emphasised that the physical
environment must be suitable for the measures applied and noted that ramps are generally
only suitable in urban areas.
Cllr. Harney stated that speed surveys have shown that the speed limit is being exceeded in
certain locations on a constant basis and stated that fixed speed cameras are a more
effective measure than traffic calming in these circumstances. He expressed the view that
a camera is required at Ardrahan village and sought information on installation costs and
monitoring arrangements. Supt. Baker responded that the costs of fixed cameras are not

the issue but the evidence base and suitability of the location are the prerequisites, and
traffic calming such as signage and ramps may be more effective in certain situations.
Cllr. Donohue commented on the loss of rural Garda stations and asked whether there were
any plans for their reopening. She commended those who attend road traffic collisions and
queried why there is seldom public feedback regarding the causes of near misses to inform
the public. She remarked on the significant costs associated with becoming a driver—
lessons, insurance, and related expenses—yet noted that driver behaviour remains
problematic. Garda Supt Baker responded that the closure of rural Garda stations stemmed
from the need to redeploy personnel and emphasised his concern regarding staffing levels
nationally, with some parts of the country now isolated as a result. He noted that station

will ultimately benefit the Galway East Division. Mr. Harley expressed the view that the
penalty points system is the most effective deterrent, as it requires drivers to always remain

that speed limit reductions should be supported through appropriate engineering measures.
Cllr. Canning stated that speed detection measures should be implemented in all areas of
noncompliance, citing Woodpark in Portumna as an example. He asked whether avenues of
communication between An Garda Siochåna and the Local Authority could be enhanced to
discuss progress on speed surveys and concerns which are submitted to An Garda Siochåna.
Cllr. Canning commented that the figure of 466 GoSafe speed van detections over 12 months
appeared low and supported the view that education is essential, suggesting that Transition
Year students should be specifically targeted. Mr. Haugh added that survey results
previously submitted to An Garda Siochåna were generally acknowledged.
Garda Supt Baker advised that he would follow up on the query regarding fixed cameras,
noting that their function and operation differ from GoSafe vans, and that not all locations
are suitable for fixed installations. He clarified that GoSafe is a contracted private operator
under An Garda Siochéna enforcement and also clarified that he would be happy to attend a
future local meeting and would circulate a dedicated email address to all councillors for
meeting related requests.
Cllr. Finnerty thanked Garda Supt Baker for his contribution, noting that most fatalities occur
in rural areas and asked whether the Garda app can detect all_vehicles without insurance
and reiterated the importance of early education for young people, including those as young
as 13. Supt Baker confirmed that accident causes cannot be disclosed publicly while cases
remain before the courts and highlighted a significant and growing concern regarding
pedestrians and cyclists using roads without any reflective gear, noting that these incidents
are particularly distressing yet avoidable. He encouraged all road users to use footpaths
where available and to wear reflective clothing for visibility.

In response to Members queries regarding the status of the Local Improvement Schemes
(LIS), Mr. Haugh advised that the scheme was not currently open for applications as there
were a significant number of applicants for non-public roads on hand. Cllr. Kelly stated he
had been informed that LIS won't be open this year or in future years as there is €5 million

allocation of €1.4m per year. He stated that representations should be made to central
government regarding the funding shortfall and this was reiterated by Cllr. Donohue. Cllr.
Kelly proposed the following Motion which was seconded by Cllr. Harney and agreed:
"That Loughrea Municipal District write to Minister Calleary's Department to request
additional funding to expedite the Local Improvement Schemes lists. "
Cllr. Kinane enquired whether the LPT Discretionary Capital Fund could be utilised to fund a
non-public road improved under the LIS scheme which subsequently deteriorated, and in
response, Mr. Harley advised that public funding cannot be used to match-fund works under
the Local Improvement Scheme.

Bridget's Road in Portumna, in the vicinity of both schools, have significantly faded and
