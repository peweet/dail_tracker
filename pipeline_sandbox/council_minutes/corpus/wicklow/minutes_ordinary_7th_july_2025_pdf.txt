ITEM NO. 12 
 
To consider the variation of the basic rate of Local Property Tax and the setting of the 
Local Adjustment Factor in accordance with the Finance (Local Property Tax) Act 2012 (as 
amended) to apply for the period as specified from the next local property tax liability 
date 1st November 2025. 
 
Elected Members were circulated with: 
 Report of Chief Executive dated 2nd July 2025 to Members on the matters to which 
Wicklow County Council shall have regard in relation to the setting of the Local 
Adjustment Factor from the Chief Executive, Ms. E. O’Gorman. 
 
Mr. B. Gleeson, Director of Services gave a presentation on Local Property Tax (LPT) and the 
setting of the Local Adjustment Factor under the following areas: 
 
 Budgetary process for Local Authorities.  
 2026 Budget process. 
 LPT Revaluation. 
 LPT 2026 Allocation. 
 Elected Members decisions. 
 The Local Property Tax (Local Adjustment Factor) Regulations 2014. 
 2026 budget adjustments. 
 Estimate of Income and expenditure. 
 Financial position of Wicklow County Council. 
 Financial effect on a liable person. 

5 
 
 
 Impact on Wicklow County Council revenue budget and service delivery for 2026. 
 Public consultation process. 
 LPT 2026 recommendation summary. 
 
In conclusion, Mr. Gleeson’s proposal is that the LPT is increased from the existing rate from 
6% to 15% and that the current rate of 6% would be maintained for the discretionary 
funding scheme within the municipal districts and that the 9% incremental increase would 
be ring-fenced for staff recruitment.   
 
An alternative option would be to only increase it to 10% rather than the full 15%.  This 
would be an incremental increase of 4%.  Mr. Gleeson also recommended to the Members 
to set the rate for the remainder lifetime of the Council, four years, up to 2029.  This can be 
altered at any stage subject to the wishes of the Members. 
 
The Elected Members discussed the matter at length, some of the issues raised were: 
 
 High cost of living. 
 Social housing delivery. 
 Revaluations welcome. 
 Response to public consultation process. 
 Public sector pay. 
 Discretionary funding. 
 Tax on the wealthy. 
 Local property tax going to footpaths, road markings etc. 
 Ring-fenced for employment of outdoor staff. 
 Ring-fenced for urban regeneration programmes. 
 
The following proposals were put forward: 
 
It was proposed by Cllr. S. Bourke, seconded by Cllr. D. Alvey:  In accordance with the 
provisions of Section 20 of the Finance (Local Property Tax) Act 2012, as amended, that the 
base rate of Local Property Tax shall stand varied upwards b 6% for one year, 2026, in 
respect of the relevant residential properties situated in the administrative area of Wicklow 
County Council. 
 
It was proposed by Cllr. D. O’Brien, seconded by Cllr. J. Behan:  In accordance with the 
provisions of section 20 of the Finance (Local Property Tax) Act 2012, as amended, the basic 
rate of Local Property Tax should stand varied downwards by 15% for one year, 2026, in 
respect of relevant residential properties situated in the administrative area of Wicklow 
County Council.   
 
It was proposed by Cllr. J. Snell and seconded by Cllr. P. Leonard:  In accordance with the 
provisions of section 20 of the Finance (Local Property Tax) Act 2012, as amended, that the 
basic rate of Local Property Tax should be maintained at the base rate for 2026 in respect of 
relevant residential properties situated in the administrative area of Wicklow County 
Council. 
 

6 
 
 
The proposal put forward by Cllr. S. Bourke and seconded by Cllr. D. Alvey ‘In accordance 
with the provisions of Section 20 of the Finance (Local Property Tax) Act 2012, as amended, 
that the base rate of Local Property Tax shall stand varied upwards b 6% for one year, 2026, 
in respect of the relevant residential properties situated in the administrative area of

ITEM NO.13 
 
To adopt the Traveller Accommodation Programme 2025 – 2029 in accordance with the 
provisions of the Traveller Accommodation Act, 1998. 
 
Mr. M. Nicholson, Director of Services, gave a short overview of the Traveller 
Accommodation Programme 2025 – 2029 outlining the following: 
 
 
 Five year programme.   
 Consultation process.   
 Wicklow County Council has a good record of providing traveller accommodation.  
  Significant shift to standard social housing as opposed to traveller specific 
accommodation. 
 Wicklow County Council also provide traveller specific accommodation for those 
who still require it.   
 Under this plan we 48 units will be provided.   
 In this five-year plan 45 units will be standard and three units will be traveller 
specific accommodation.   
 Fire safety improvements.   
 
A short discussion followed in which the Elected Members raised queries which were 
responded to by Mr. Nicholson.  It was agreed the mid-term review of the programme 
would place more focus on youth consultation.

ITEM NO. 19 
 
Notice of Motion number 1 
 
Notice of motion in the name of Cllr. C. Winstanley (proposer), seconded by Cllr. T. 
Fortune and supported by Cllrs. D. Alvey and M. Barry (29th May 2025) 
 
‘It has now been a year since Ireland officially recognised the State of Palestine, and nearly 
two years since the beginning of Israel’s ongoing military campaign in Gaza. 
 
In light of this, Elected Members of Wicklow County Council call upon the Chief Executive of 
the Council to write to the Government on our behalf, urging immediate and decisive action. 
We request that the Government act to end the facilitation and sale of Israel Bonds under its 
authority, and that the Central Bank of Ireland cease all regulatory oversight of these 
financial instruments, which currently supports continued violence and destruction. 
 

10 
 
 
Continued regulation and facilitation of these bonds represents not only a moral failure but 
also a clear violation of Ireland’s legal obligations under: 
 
The UN Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide, 
International human rights law, and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (CFT) legislation. 
 
As a nation that upholds a position of military neutrality, Ireland must not be complicit in 
enabling financial mechanisms that fund or support potential war crimes. Unless there is an 
immediate and verifiable ceasefire, the sale and regulation of Israel Bonds must end. 
We urge the Irish Government to take a stand and ensure that our country plays no role in 
facilitating further violence or injustice in the region’. 
 
A short discussion followed.  The motion was passed by all present.
