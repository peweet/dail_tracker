3. Correspondence 
 
(a) Healthy Ireland Framework Consultation Campaign 2026-2036 
The Chief Officer informed members that the Department of Health is currently undertaking a public 
consultation on the new Healthy Ireland Framework. They are inviting organisations and their 
networks to share their views and help shape the future direction of health and wellbeing policy in 
Ireland.  
The consultation is open until 22nd April and submissions can be made online via the following link: 
https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-health/consultations/healthy-ireland-framework-public-
consultation/ 
The Chief Officer asked for all LCDC members to encourage their own organisations to participate and 
make a contribution. 
(b) Community Development Quality Standards Framework 
The Chief Officer advised that the DRCDG held an online information session on the draft 
framework for Chief Officers on Monday 23rd March at 11am. The purpose of the online 
information session is to provide background information on the Quality Standards 
Framework and next steps and to provide any clarifications necessary. Ms. Ireland explained 
that she was unable to attend this information session but has since reached out to the 
DRCDG seeking documentation.     
 
4. LECP Presentation: Age Friendly Laois 
 
Ms. Danielle Keane, Acting Age Friendly Laois Co-ordinator, provided members with a presentation of the 
following updates from their five actions contained in the Local Community and Development Plan (LECP).

Action 5.2.4.  
“Review the ‘Laois Connects’ Programme to deliver an annual schedule of events that promote health and 
wellbeing events and supports throughout the county and investigate innovative ways of engaging 
communities in activities”. 
 
Update 
In terms of health and wellbeing, Laois Connects promotes health and wellbeing activities during Mental 
Health week in October, highlighting the benefits of staying connected and engaged in your community. 
The theme for October 2025 was “Community” and activities included:

•

•

•

•

Age Friendly Laois also promotes the ongoing lunch clubs in Laois. Quarterly lunch clubs in Camross and 
Portlaoise. 
 
 
Action 5.2.6.  
“Increase participation in Laois Older Peoples Council (OPC) and actively seek to achieve diversity, equality, 
and inclusiveness.” 
 
Update  
The OPC Executive is made up of 8 members.  
All Executive members are female, and we are looking to engage with men to balance genders. The wider 
OPC council of approximately 400 contacts is made up of about 75% female and 25% male.

Action 5.3.4.  
“Support Age Friendly community groups in the hosting of events locally including the Age Friendly Laois 
AGM.” 
 
 

4 
 
Update  
AGM will return to a standalone event in 2026, as it has been incorporated into the Live Well Expo in 2024 
and 2025.  
On request, Age Friendly Laois have provided support to Age friendly communities such as the Heath 
Active Retirement group in Q4, 2025. Previous activities supported included Bridge Clubs, Bowling and 
Variety shows. 
 
 
Action 9.1.4. 
“Support initiatives and schemes that aim to reduce isolation and improve safety and security through 
community text alert and community development schemes and projects e.g. smoke alarms for older 
people, carbon monoxide alarms.” 
 
Update 
In Q4, 2025, Age Friendly Laois collaborated with HSE, An Gardai Siochana, Muintir na Tíre and LOETB to 
develop a severe weather guide. This was launched at an event in Laois Partnership company offices in 
November 2025. The Guide is being distributed via the individual partners involved with Age Friendly Laois 
distributing 5000 to date through Laois County Libraries and Laois County Fire Service.  
Age Friendly Laois continue to promote Age Friendly events, including the Lunch Clubs being delivered by 
Laois Partnership Company. We continue to promote all safety initiatives via our networks and channels. 
 
The members congratulated the entire Age Friendly Laois team on the wonderful work and initiatives 
being rolled out. There was unanimous agreement about the importance of being able to easily engage 
with elderly and isolated people in County Laois. Mr. Michael Rainey, Chief Executive Officer, Laois County 
Council, advised that he is aware that some counties have already established “Social Connectors” who 
can liaise with and work closely with all the various organisations and services. The Chief Officer agreed to 
investigate and explore this a little further to see if something similar could be done in Laois.
