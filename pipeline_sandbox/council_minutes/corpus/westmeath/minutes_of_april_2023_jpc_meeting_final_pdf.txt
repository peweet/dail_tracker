Report from An Garda Síochána: 
 
Chief Supt. John Dollard reported to the meeting, drawing attention to various crime statistics in 
the county, as contained in the Garda Report.  He then asked Detective Inspector Tom Quinn to 
go through the report in greater detail.  
 
CRIME 
 
Property crime had increased from 313 in 2022 (Q1) to 424 in 2023 (Q1), divisional totals.  
In Athlone, figures were up from 146 (Q1) in 2022 to 267 (Q1) in 2023.   
For Mullingar, they were down from 167 in 2022 (Q1) to 157 (Q1) in 2023.   
 
In terms of burglary, the rate decreased from 59 in Q1 2022 to 40 in Q1 2023, a 32% divisional 
decrease.    
 
The number of thefts from vehicles for the Athlone District fell from 12 in Q1 2022 to 7 in Q1 
2023 a decrease of 42%.  In Mullingar District, the figures also dropped from 19 in Q1 2022 to 9 
in Q1 2023, a 53% decrease. 
 
The rate of theft and unauthorised taking of vehicles at divisional level increased from 9 in Q1 
2022 to 13 Q1 in 2023, a 44% divisional decrease.    
 
There was a 66% increase in the number of reported thefts from shops, in Athlone, figures 
increased from 53 in Q1 2022 to 131 in Q1 2023.  For Mullingar, figures also increased from 75 in 
Q1 2022 to 81 in Q1 2023.   
 
There was an increase in the number of reported thefts of other property, 63 in Q1 2022 to 107 
in Q1 2022. (Divisional totals). 

3 
 
 
DRUGS 
 
The following statistics for drugs crime were noted. 
In the Athlone Division, incidents of Section 3 (possession of drugs for personal use) violations 
went from 19 in Q1 2022 to 30 Q1 in 2023, an increase of 58%, while incidents of Section 15 
(possession of a drug with intent for sale or supply) went from 6 in Q1 2022 to 9 in Q1 2023. 
 
In the Mullingar Division, incidents of Section 3 violations went from 35 in Q1 2021 to 15 in Q1 
2023, a decrease of 57%, while incidents of Section 15 went from 2 in Q1 2022 to 6 in Q1 2023. 
 
Chief Supt. John Dollard updated the JPC Members on the following Community Engagements: 
 
• 
Property Marking Day: On the 4 March 2023 a Property Marking Day was organised 
in Mullingar District in conjunction with the Public Participation Network of 
Westmeath County Council. This took place at Multyfarnham GAA Club and the day 
was a success with property such as strimmers, lawnmowers, large cattle trailers 
and other types of property marked on the day with the owners’ Eircode.   
 
• 
Transition Year Work Experience: Community Policing were delighted to hold two 
weeks of work experience for 32 TY students. The TY experience consists of visits to 
the Circuit Court, the Midlands Prison, The Garda Museum, Collins Barrack, and 
Templemore Garda Training College as well as seeing the day to day working of a 
Garda Station. The feedback is always positive and large numbers of students apply 
for this work experience placement each year. 
 
• 
EYE Project Pool Competition: The annual pool competition between An Garda 
Síochána and the Eye Project attendees took place on 23rd February 2023. This is a 
fun event designed to break down barriers between Gardaí and the youths and build 
rapport and trust.  The Gardai paid tribute to Joan O’Connor, the leader of the EYE 
Project in Mullingar, for her input into this worthwhile programme. 
 
• 
Mullingar Pride March takes place on 8th July 2023 in Mullingar and is an important 
way of displaying inclusivity of all sectors of society.  
 
• 
Delivery of Easter eggs: Community Policing delivered Easter Eggs to the homes of 
Little Blue Hero’s and the Active Age Community 
 
• 
Business Watch: Launch of Business Watch for Athlone Town, carried out in 
conjunction with Athlone Town Chamber.

The Chairman thanked Chief Supt. John Dollard, Det. Insp. Tom Quinn, Supt. Michelle Baker, and 
Supt. David Nolan for their presentations.    
 
Members raised some queries which were responded to by the Gardai. 
 
Chief Supt. John Dollard informed the Members that there was a worrying spike in the theft of 
vehicles especially imported cars from Japan.  Chief Supt. Dollard explained that this was that 
case because the cars do not come with immobilisers, making them more susceptible to being 
broken into or stolen. 
 
Chief Supt. Dollard also informed the Members that there was a large reduction in the number 
of residential burglaries since the January 2023 JPC Meeting.  
 
In addition, he informed the Meeting that more people are now coming forward to local Gardaí 
to report sexual crime, stating that there has been an increase in the reporting of such crime. 
 
 
Cllr John Dolan raised the issue of “organised begging” in Athlone and wanted to know if it might 
assist the Gardaí in tackling this issue if the Local Authority introduced byelaws to deal with 
people begging on the streets in Athlone.  He stated that anecdotal evidence suggests that these 
people are being brought into the town by bus to beg for the day. 
 
In response, Supt. Michelle Baker asked that if the Members or members of the public had any 
knowledge or evidence of persons being brought into Athlone by bus to beg on the streets, that 
they should come forward and inform the Gardai. Supt. Baker indicated that the current 
legislative framework, without byelaws, is adequate to deal with this issue. 
 
Cllr Dolan also raised concerns regarding CCTV on the Greenway and the possibility of a public 
defibrillator being placed outside the Athlone Garda Station.   Cllr Dolan asked if the cost of this 
defibrillator might be funded by Garda resources.  
 

5 
 
Chief Supt. John Dollard informed the Members that the big issue with the CCTV relates to its use 
in public areas and the need to “manage privacy” but he did state it plays a very important role 
in the prevention and detection of crime. 
 
Chief Supt. Dollard advised the Members that he sits on an advisory board which makes 
recommendations to the Garda Commissioner about the use of CCTV and therefore any 
application received for CCTV on the Greenway will be supported by him. 
 
Regarding defibrillators, he stated that more and more members of the Gardaí are being trained 
in their use and he is not opposed to the idea of a public defibrillator being made available outside 
Garda Stations.
