C/312/25 
HOUSING CAPITAL UPDATE   
Members considered the report circulated with the agenda in relation to 
the above. 
 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer, provided an update to members 
on the status of the various housing projects since the 30th June 2025. 
This included an analysis of projects that had been completed, 
currently on site or at tender stage together with those at pre-planning, 
planning, and detailed design stage. This also included an overview of 
the various funding submissions that had been submitted to the 
Department of Housing for stage approval with the aim of advancing 
eighteen projects county-wide and delivering 552 social housing units.  
 
Mr Gallagher advised also in relation to the completions, tenders 
advertised and works started since the 30th June 2025. Members were 
informed of the status of the social housing delivery programme and 
the fact that 1402 housing units were now in the pipeline. Details were 
provided as follows:-

The meeting was then opened to the floor and the following issues 
raised. 
 
Issues  
• General appreciation for the range of works completed to date 
and in the pipeline all of which has been facilitated by the 
Housing Capital team. Recognition that the hard work and 
dedication of the housing team have contributed to this. 
• That it might be more beneficial to have the meetings on a six 
weekly or two monthly basis in the interest of more 
comprehensive updates and to allow for the completion of 
works. 
• Clarification requested in relation to the SHIP Renewal process 
and the implications for towns and villages including the 
methodology for identifying such buildings. Specific reference 
made to the village of Carrick. General welcome for the Old 
Fintra Road site.  
• Traffic Light System for development must be utilised where 
there are available sites and families need to be able to have 
access to accommodation. Use of ex Government and HSE 
buildings should be considered. 
• Smaller towns and Villages need to be accommodated when 
planning for housing development. Notes associated 
infrastructure available in the villages of Pettigo and 
Mountcharles. 
• General welcome for the High Road Project and the fact that the 
S.I process is moving forward. 
• Cited the need for the utmost transparency in terms of the 
concrete, aggregate being used in social housing.  
• Use of vacant HSE property in Pettigo should be followed up. 
• Concern expressed that people from other areas of the county 
were being placed in homeless accommodation in Bundoran 
and that this was not always the most practical solution.  
• Housing provision on the Islands must not be forgotten about.  
• Uisce Éireann service provision a key element of the housing 
development process. Noted specific issues in Falcarragh. 
• Access issues on the N56 are impacting development and the 
provision of S.Is. 
• Government policy responsible for the lack of housing.

Follow-Up 
Cllr Mc Clafferty proposed, seconded by Cllr Kelly that correspondence 
issue again regarding the request for a meeting with the Department in 
relation to access issues on the N56. 
 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer drew members attention to the 
fact that other housing colleagues and himself regularly attended MD 
meetings and workshops. He said that a number of the issues raised 
during the meeting could be dealt with at this level. He advised 
members of the following:- 
 
That it would be possible to look at opportunities under the SHIP 
Renewal Scheme and that this could be done in conjunction with the 
Regeneration Team where there were viable options. Noted that there 

 
6 
 
was ongoing engagement with Patricia Mc Intyre and the Regeneration 
Team in relation to specific opportunities and where properties were 
viable to renovate. Housing, he said, would collaborate with members 
to identify opportunities and drew attention to the fact that there was an 
open expression of interest portal on the website for interested parties. 
It was noted that excellent use had been made of the old Garda Station 
in Malin and that options could be explored in relation to the buildings 
referenced in Pettigo and Mountcharles. He said that there were 
challenges with Town Centre development particularly in terms of 
parking and the provision of same.  
 
Mr Gallagher said that there was a traffic light system in place as prior 
to any proposed development taking place items such as access to 
water, sewerage, disposal of surface water and other environmental 
considerations etc were looked at. This process was also used, he 
advised, when looking at acquiring lands. 
 
Engagement, he confirmed, would continue in relation to development 
in the smaller towns.  
 
Members were informed that there was ongoing engagement with 
Uisce Éireann regarding all projects and that scheduled workshops 
would take place in September for each of the Municipal Districts. 
 
Regarding materials used in aggregate and concrete, he said that the 
Council worked to their own specification on all projects. The materials 
used, aggregates, concrete etc were covered, he advised, by this 
specification. All building contractors, it was noted were working to this 
at the minute. Detailed testing was also, he added, part of the 
specification and certification for supplies was available from the 
supplier and quarries concerned in line with the Construction Product 
regulations   
 
Cllr Coyle was informed that a report would be provided in relation to 
the Killylastin development and that this could be presented at MD 
level.  
 
It was confirmed that response times varied for individual submissions 
and that this also depended on the scale and complexity of the project 
concerned.  
 
Cllr Mc Mahon was informed that would be possible to follow-up with 
the developer in Ballyshannon. It was confirmed that contact would 
also be made with the HSE in relation to the property in Pettigo. 
 
Continuing Mr Gallagher said that he would be happy to discuss the 
situation in Fintown at the Glenties MD meeting given that the 
feasibility of that project was being reviewed. 

 
7 
 
Issues in relation to sewerage provision in Derrybeg had, he advised, 
been raised with Uisce Éireann and with the Department and would 
continue to be highlighted. 
 
Regarding the query on departmental approvals and associated 
response time, he said that the Housing team would continue to 
consult with members in relation to projects where there were undue 
delays. 
 
Concluding, he confirmed that the Council was bound by departmental 
budgets and the relevant thresholds in respect of the Second Hand 
Acquisition scheme. 
 
Mr Eamonn Brown, Divisional Manager, Housing & Corporate said that 
expressions of interest had been sought in terms of proposed 
emergency accommodation county-wide. Twenty-seven submission, 
he said, had been received and it was anticipated that other locations 
would come on stream into the future. It was, he said, imperative that 
the quality of the accommodation was sufficient so as to facilitate 
overnight supervision, and 24 hour access to the property including the 
provision of breakfast etc. Members would, he confirmed, be kept 
informed of developments. 
 
He alluded to the fact that the Homeless Action Team meetings were 
held monthly and included inter-agency involvement where specific 
cases were able to be brought to the attention of the HSE. He said that 
that when people were being moved a distance consideration was 
given to those who did not have a particular tie or need to stay in a 
particular location. The policy, he added, was not to uproot families. 
Bed and Breakfast vouchers, he said, were still in operation for those 
presenting as homeless. 
 
Mr Brown said that he would liaise with the Area Manager Housing in 
the Letterkenny/Milford MD with regard to the circulation of the most up 
to date report on housing need in the Letterkenny area. A similar 
report, he advised, was available for each Municipal District. 
 
On the issue of Approved Housing Bodies, he confirmed, that the 
Council was not able to show favour to any developer but that the Irish 
Council for Social Housing was the umbrella organisation for AHBs. 
Interested developers, he advised, should be directed to contact the 
ICSH who would then liaise with the relevant Approved Housing Body. 
 
Concluding, he said that a response had been received from the 
Department of Housing, Local Government & Heritage in relation to 
Second Hand Acquisitions and that the €2m allocation on the 1st April 
was the final allocation for 2025. Going forward, he said, that the 
primary focus was on new house construction by Local Authorities and 
AHBs.

The Cathaoirleach thanked members for their co-operation and 
contributions and said that if there were specific queries that they 
should be directed to the senior management team directly.  
 
 
This concluded the business of the meeting.
