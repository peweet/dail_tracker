 TO DISCUSS THE ONGOING FUEL AND COST OF LIVING CRISIS. 
Cllr Devine said that it was important that a discussion took place as 
the ongoing fuel and cost of living crisis had implications for all sections 
of society. He said that he wanted to put forward a motion of no 
confidence in the current Government in light of its failure to address 
the needs of the people during the current crisis.  
 
The Cathaoirleach responding said that his role was to protect Donegal 
County Council and do what was in its best interests. This county, he 
advised depended on national government for funding. He opened the 
meeting to the floor and said that he was not prepared to facilitate a 
motion of no confidence in the current government. 
 
Several members expressed grave disappointment at this  
 
Mr Paul Mc Gill, Meetings Administrator responding to several queries 
confirmed that notification of the meeting had issued on Thursday 23rd 
April and within the required statutory timeframe. 
 
He noted also that the issue of a motion under urgent business did not 
arise as the meeting had been called on foot of a requisition from the  
Cathaoirleach which had identified the item to be considered. 
  
Cllr Mc Monagle said that it was treading on dangerous ground by not 
accepting the motion of no confidence.  
 
A number of members concurred with this assessment and highlighted 
a range of issues within the county which they contended clearly 
showed that Donegal had been left behind in terms of infrastructural, 
economic and social development. Failure to adequately address the 
defective concrete block crisis was also referenced. They highlighted 

3 
 
the lack of investment and the need to ensure the equal distribution of 
any available funding. 
 
Cllr Jordan asked the Cathaoirleach to reconsider his stance on the 
matter.  
 
Cllr Mc Bride said that he believed that the Government had misread 
the level of discontent and had lost touch with the general public. 
 
Cllr C Brogan said that it was easy to be selective and bash the 
Government, but that considerable investment had been secured for 
the county. He acknowledged also the role that successive cross-party 
delegations had played in securing funding at national level. Energy, he 
said, needed to be focused on getting things done and concentrating 
instead on what the Council could actually control. He said that such 
negativity was not helpful and that a constructive approach was 
required going forward.  
 
A divisive approach, he contended, was not in the best interest of the 
people of Donegal going forward. 
 
Cllr Harley noted that the fuel crisis was not of the government’s 
making and reiterated the need to be mindful of the level of funding that 
actually came into the county. 
 
Cllr Blaney noted that the DCB Scheme was working for many people 
who were getting their homes rebuilt. He said that some of the 
commentary today served only to encourage people not to apply.  
 
 
 
 
A heated debate then took place and the Cathaoirleach declared the 
meeting closed.  
 
 
 
This concluded the business of the meeting.
