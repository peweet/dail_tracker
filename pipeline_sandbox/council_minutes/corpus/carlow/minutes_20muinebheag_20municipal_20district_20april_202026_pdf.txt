Making Spaces for and with Teenagers Initiatives —
Fairgreen, Muinebheag
Barry Knowles gave a comprehensive presentation. He was delighted to
inform the meeting that Carlow County Council has secured funding of €50,000
to develop a dedicated teen space within a section of the Fairgreen,
Muinebheag. Only six local authorities were awarded funding and Carlow
County Council is one of the successful applicants. This is a fantastic
opportunity and very positive for the whole community.
He advised that a National Pilot Programme was introduced in 2025, the Making
Spaces for and with Teenagers Initiative, funded through the Dormant Accounts
Fund. This initiative was established to address a clear gap identified across
the country — the need for safe, inclusive and youth-friendly public spaces
specifically designed for teenagers. A key feature of the programme is that
these spaces must be co-designed with young people, ensuring their voices,
ideas and lived experiences directly influence how the space looks, feels and
functions. The overall ajm is to support social connection, wellbeing and a
stronger sense of belonging among teenagers by giving them a meaningful roie
in shaping their communities.
Young people from the area played a central role in shaping the design,
identifying the features they value most and ensuring the space reflects their
needs and aspirations. The features include sheltered seating, accessible
seating, outdoor gaming tables, smart bench with WiFi capability and in-
ground trampoline. Their involvement was instrumental in securing the
funding.

Anti-littering Update — Environment
Liam Carroll, Senior Executive Officer, Environment, gave an update on the
use of CCTV which was introduced in October 2025.
New legislation under GDPR now permits CCTV to be used to identify persons
committing offences under Litter Pollution and Waste Management
legislation. He emphasised that it was a complicated process to roll out, and
before cameras can be installed at any site, approval is required from en
oversight board and the Chief Executive.
He also advised that the use of CCTV, which is managed by a third party is
expensive and wouldn't be possible without the assistance of VVERLA funding.
6 locations, with rotating cameras, have been installed since last October. 58
fines have been issued to date, and 6 are before the court for non-payment.

Any proposed location must have tangible, consistent evidence oi dumping,
and the Environment department must be very clear on why cameras are
required. Further sites will be brought to this oversight board in May/June for
approval, and Liam encouraged the Members to contact him with feedback and
welcomed any suggested locations that they may have.
Liam explained to the Members how rogue dumping and fly tipping in remote
rural locations such as field entrance gates as well as the Mount Leinster area
are a huge problem. He also highlighted how the Environment department
liaise with active groups in the area, and work closely with Coillte in the battle
against littering and fly tipping. Incidents of large scale illegal durnping hae
also been recently discovered in the Leighlin and Ballinabranna areas.
