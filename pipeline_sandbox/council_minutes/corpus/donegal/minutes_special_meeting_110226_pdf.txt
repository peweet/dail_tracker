C/58/26 
 TO APPROVE THE CONSTRUCTION OF 5 NO SOCIAL HOUSING 
UNITS CONSISTING OF 2 NO 1 BED APARTMENTS, 2 NO 2 BED 
HOUSES AND 1 NO 3 BED HOUSE AT COSÁN NA NIARLAÍ, 
RATHMULLAN 
 
 Members considered the report circulated with the agenda in relation to 
the above.  
 
 
 
 On the proposal of Cllr Mc Garvey, seconded by Cllr Blaney the 
construction of 5 no social housing units consisting of 2 no. 1 bed 
apartments, 2 no. 2 bed houses and 1 no. 3 bed house at Cosán na 
nÍarlaí, Rathmullan was approved, in accordance with Part XI of the 
Planning & Development Act, 2000 (as amended) and Part 8 of the 
Planning & Development Regulations 2001(as amended). 
 

 
 
 
 
 Cllr Mc Garvey welcomed the development and thanked the Housing 
team for their hard work to date. He said there was an increasing 
demand for housing in Rathmullan and cited the need for further 
developments of this nature. Cllrs Blaney & Meehan were in agreement 
and said that the pressure needed to be maintained given the numbers 
on the waiting list. 
 
C/59/26 
 HOUSING CAPITAL UPDATE 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer, Housing Capital briefed members 
on the progress since the last meeting on the 28th November 2025. 
This included projects completed in the period 2022-2025, projects on 
site and projects at tender stage, together with projects at planning & 
detailed design stage and those being progressed through competitive 
dialogue. 
 
Members were informed that the current status of social housing 
delivery in the county was as follows but did not include AHBs which 
remain subject to approval:

(incl acquisitions) 
 
403 
 
Social Housing Units On Site  
 
466 
 
Projects at Tender Stage  
 
69 
In- House Projects with Planning 
Approved/ Detailed Design   
 
254 
Competitive Dialogue Projects 
with Planning & UE Approval  
 
259 
 
A lengthy debate ensued during which members raised several issues. 
 
Mr John Gallagher, Senior Engineer, responding to a number of 
queries in relation to Uisce Éireann capacity and water supply in 
Letterkenny noted that there was ongoing interaction with Uisce 
Éireann who were currently reviewing the existing supply and looking at 
the capacity for new developments in the area. 
 
It was noted that over 80 settlements across the county were being 
looked at in terms of the delivery of social housing. Work was ongoing, 
he added, and solution being looked at in terms of the various issues 
that arose.  

 
 
There was, he confirmed, land allocated in Rockytown Phase 3 for the 
delivery of a group home and that a co-ordinated approach was needed 
and wrap around package identified and secured. The issue of group 
homes was one, he said, that was discussed on an ongoing basis by 
the Housing Disability Strategy Group.  
 
With regard to the fast tracking system for housing delivery in terms of 
the single stage application process as compared to the stage 4 
process, he noted that considerable work had been done by Eilish Mc 
Laughlin in relation to this aspect and that there had been ongoing 
interaction with the Department. The single stage approval process was 
he said trying to amalgamate all into a one stage approval. 
 
The Council, he confirmed, was working hard on acquiring lands in 
Ramelton, Carrigart and Milford and was progressing development on 
same. He said that further discussion could be had at MD level and 
consultation sought with the Roads & Planning Divisions as to how best 
to move forward in these areas. 
 
The competitive dialogue process, he noted, was competitive in terms 
of developers submitting applications and that there were commercial 
sensitivities associated with same. He said that he would liaise with Cllr 
Farren in relation to his query on North Inishowen. 
 
Every effort, he said, was being made to move forward with the various 
methods of social housing delivery and that this would continue as a 
matter of urgency. The Housing Capital team, he said, attended MD 
meetings and there was an opportunity there to raise issues at local 
level. 
 
He said that further detail could be provided in terms of queries raised 
on the provision of childcare facilities. 
 
C/60/26 
 FLOOD RELIEF SCHEMES IN DONEGAL 
Mr Seamus Hopkins, Senior Engineer, addressing members advised 
that there were 119 flood relief schemes contained within the Flood 
Relief Masterplan with Donegal having 15 schemes in total. He said 
that a dedicated unit was in place within Donegal County Council and 
was 100% funded by the OPW. This unit, he stated, worked solely on 
the delivery of capital schemes and was currently leading out on  9 no. 
schemes in three bundles and providing support to two schemes lead 
by the OPW. Quarterly reports were provided, he said, and a newsletter 
published on the scheme website.  

 
 
He proceeded to give an overview of the various flood relief scheme 
stages and the current status of the projects in hand including those 
being led out by the OPW.  
 
Donegal County Council and the OPW in partnership were, he 
confirmed working to deliver 12 schemes in Donegal with 3 to be 
submitted for planning consent in 2026. Options also existed, he said, 
to explore alternative delivery routes using dedicated pilot schemes.  
 
Cllr C Brogan outlined the need for more discussion on the matter at 
MD level as members were all aware of serious issues in their own 
localities which needed interaction and debate. He proposed that this 
be initiated at MD level and the National Parks & Wildlife together with 
the OPW be invited to be part of this interaction.  
 
Cllr Meehan was informed that CFRAM maps were available online and 
that national flooding maps both provided information on lands that 
could not be used due to the flooding risk. 
 
Mr Shane Mc Monagle, Senior Executive Engineer, said that the 
Strategic Flood Risk Assessment carried out by the Council was in line 
with what was required and informed what the planners looked at when 
considering applications for planning permission. Climate change, he 
said, had been worked into where housing could be located in the 
county. Planners also used, he advised, a justification test as part of 
this process especially in relation the urban areas.  
 
Several issues were raised in the ensuing debate. 
 
Cllr Mc Clafferty alluding to planning policy in general asked if 
arrangements had been put in place for a workshop to discuss the Irish 
language requirements for Gaeltacht housing. It was unanimously 
agreed to hold a plenary workshop on Monday 23rd March 2026 at 2pm 
in the County House, Lifford to discuss same.  
 
Mr Seamus Hopkins, Senior Engineer, said that the calculation in 
relation to the damage level assessment was blunt but that changes 
had been made in consultation with the OPW, and these included some 
consideration for community benefit, cognisance of wider social issues 
and room for further consideration where schemes were borderline. He 
said that overall, the cost benefit was taken into consideration and that 
small and marginal schemes were looked at using this criteria.  
He informed Cllr Harley that he would liaise directly with him in relation 
to the issue with the CPI Centre in Castlefin.  

 
 
It was acknowledged that there was ongoing engagement with the 
farming community and that this involved several pilot schemes and 
initiatives. Inishowen Rivers Trust, he added, had been actively 
involved with the farming community in relation to the installation of 
leaky dams.  
 
The OPW, he said relied on the Arterial Drainage Act, but that 
consideration was being given at present to reviewing the legislation in 
relation to drainage and short circuiting some of the current 
impediments. There was a need also, he said, to ensure that any 
schemes submitted were of the optimum value. There was no room or 
value, he added, in carrying out piecemeal works which could damage 
the overall scheme in the long run. There was an awareness at board 
level, he said, that these schemes needed to be progressed at a swifter 
pace. Another development, he said, was that the OPW now allowed 
the detailed design to proceed whilst planning permission was being 
considered.  
 
In relation to the queries on Pettigo, he noted , that Donegal County 
Council simply delivered the schemes on behalf of the OPW. Arising 
from the recent ministerial visit, he said, that a commitment had been 
given to meet with the flooding agency in Northern Ireland to consider 
proposals. Any scheme, he said would have to be initiated in the first 
instance by the OPW.  
 
With regard to the issue in Clyhore, he confirmed that he would ask the 
team together with the local Roads Division to look at the issues raised.  
Area Roads teams, he said, did carry out drainage and remedial works 
within the designated budgetary frameworks.  
 
Schemes in Donegal Town and Letterkenny, it was confirmed, were 
ongoing as part of a national pilot project.  Mr Hopkins said that the 
team were tendering out topographical surveys in relation to the land 
itself, identifying water metres, water courses, CCTV surveys etc  so 
that as much information as possible could go into the tender 
documents so as to short circuit the process. 
 
It was hoped, he said, to engage more at local level as the flood relief 
team had doubled in size and that additional resources would be 
sought from the OPW. Flooding was the biggest climate action risk that 
Donegal faced, he contended, and thus there was a need for a robust 
and well-resourced flood relief unit.  

 
 
On the issue of bridges, he said, there were, challenges in terms of 
carrying out the necessary works. He alluded also to the impact of EU 
legislation which could not be short circuited. 
 
Cllr Kelly said that all stakeholders needed to work together and 
supported Cllr Brogan’s call for the OPW and Inland Fisheries Ireland 
to meet with the flood relief team and the elected members to discuss 
the situation in Donegal going forward.  
 
Cllr Scanlon called on the Roads team to look at our bridge structures 
in terms of debris building up in the arches. He noted that an 
application could be made to the Lough’s Agency for the removal of 
gravel in riverbeds and suggested that use could be made of this 
process to clear many of our rivers.  
 
Cllr Naughton was informed that clarity would be provided outside of 
the meeting in relation to his query on what aspects of drainage would 
be able to carried out by the local farming community.
