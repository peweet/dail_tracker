ITEM NO. 20 
 
To note Wicklow County Council 2025 Countywide Differential Rent Scheme for social 
housing tenants. 
 
 
Mr. M. Nicholson, Director of Services advised the Elected Members of the following: 
 

10 
 
 
 Wicklow County Council charge rents on Council properties and the rent is based 
entirely on income.   
 The Council are obliged to review the rent scheme and the rents being charged on a 
regular basis. 
 The Council have two rent schemes in the county.   
 Bray has had its own rent scheme historically. 
 last audit report instructed the Council to have one scheme for the county. 
 Wicklow County Council has made a couple of minor amendments to the scheme.  
Carers and maintenance payments are excluded.  The minimum rent proposed is 
going to €30 per week.   
 In order to assess the possible effect of moving the Bray rents to the County Council 
scheme a random sample of 150 tenants was taken and assessed based on the rent 
scheme of the County Council. The majority of the Bray tenants will be slightly 
better as a result of nuances between the Bray scheme and the County Council 
scheme.  
 The Differential Rent Scheme is an Executive function of the Council.  The role of 
the Elected Member is to note the scheme. While the Local Government Reform 
Act 2014 restates that the scheme as a reserved function, under the schedule it also 
refers to the grounding legislation  Section 31 in the Housing (Miscellaneous 
Provisions) Act 2009, which has not yet commenced.  
 
There followed a broad discussion in which the Elected Members voiced their concerns in 
respect of their role in relation to this matter.  Cllr. J. Behan, seconded by Cllr. J. Snell 
proposed seeking independent legal advice to clarify if this is a reserved function as listed in 
the Local Government Reform Act 2014. 
 
It was agreed by all to defer this item until a second legal opinion has been received.

ITEM NO. 27 
 
Notice of Motions 
 
Notice of motion in the name of Cllr. J. Behan (proposer), seconded by Cllr. P. Leonard (4th 
March 2025). 
 
‘That this Council negotiates with the HSE the purchase of the building in Enniskerry known 
as ‘Curran’. 
 
This building has been vacant for the past 20 years and was used in the past as sheltered 
accommodation for elderly Enniskerry residents.  They believe it would accommodate 4 
residents and a carer.  They also believe that funds might be accessed through the Vacant 
Property Refurbishment Grant’. 
 
Response 
 
WCC has made contact with the HSE regarding the property Curam, Enniskerry, Co. Wicklow 
 
WCC have studied the HSE report and have the following comments; according to the 
structural survey there are a number of issues regarding the property:- 
 
• 
Significant structural cracking at the front and rear of the house. This is likely to 
involve some level of underpinning and will require extensive opening up work in 

12 
 
 
advance to ascertain the most appropriate solution. Sealed vertical joints will need 
to be provided externally. 
• 
Timber decay generally throughout the house is likely to be a significant problem and 
will require further investigation and opening up. There will be significant costs 
associated with timber replacement and treatment and with temporary propping. 
• 
At ground floor, the replacement of the timber flooring construction with an 
insulated damp protected ground floor slab. 
• 
Damp protection at ground floor will be required. This is particularly the case at the 
lower ground floor level at the rear of the house, which will require an element of 
tanking. 
• 
Significant work is required at roof level, including works to the gutter, fixing of 
slates and probably (at least) partial re-roofing of the building which has complex 
roof geometry. 
• 
If WCC were to acquire this property it would be expected that the costs of 
refurbishment to this property would be significant.  The building is question is also a 
listed structure. 
 
The Local Authority therefore is not in a position to acquire this property. In addition if the 
Local Authority acquired the property it would have to go through a process to select an 
appropriate Approved Housing Body. 
 
An Approved Housing Body acquiring the property would face the same issues and costs 
outlined above.
