C/316/25 
HOUSING CAPITAL UPDATE  
Members considered the report circulated with the agenda in relation to 
the above. 
 
Mr Donal Walker, Senior Engineer provided an update on the status of 
the various social housing projects since the last Special Meeting on 
the 25th July 2025. This included detail on projects which had 
commenced, those that had been completed and those currently 
awaiting planning approval. An overview of those at tender stage was 
also provided.  
 
Mr Jim Boner, Senior Executive Engineer gave an overview of the 
various projects onsite together with a status update on projects 
currently at tender stage. 
 
Ms Claire Burke, Senior Executive Architect outlined the status of 
projects at planning and detailed design stage. An analysis was also 
provided in relation to the various projects at pre-planning stage. 

4 
 
Ms Donna Mc Groarty, Area Manager, Housing Design/ Construction 
highlighted and presented information on a range of projects being 
pursued through the competitive dialogue process. 
 
Mr Patsy Lafferty, Director Housing, Corporate & Cultural Services said 
that the focus going forward would be on updating members on the 
progress made since the previous meeting and as to where the various 
housing projects were at.  
 
Issues Raised 
• Housing team complimented on their work and effort to date in 
moving forward the housing programme. 
• Concern raised that the net need of 1300 units is inaccurate 
given that there are currently 1300 people on the waiting list in 
Letterkenny.  
• Suggested that homelessness is not being adequately 
addressed especially in the Glenties Municipal District. People 
are emigrating as they cannot access housing.  
• Development at Hughie Joes, Gaoth Dobhair – capacity there 
for further development if the Council did the sewerage 
connection works themselves.  
• High rents and Defective Concrete Blocks issue are having a 
major impact on the housing crisis. 
• Letterkenny needs additional housing units.  
• Need to ensure that housing for people with disabilities is 
prioritised and that adaptation works are progressed as people 
with disabilities are being excluded from housing.  
• General welcome for the progress with S.I Housing. 
• Highlighted the fact that a site had been identified in Killybegs 
during discussions on the Local Area Plan as being suitable for 
older people’s housing given the flat nature of the site and its 
proximity to the town centre. Request that this be kept in mind 
going forward. 
• Noted that temporary works had been carried out on several 
houses in the Ard na Gualla Duibhe site in Moville and that this 
was to be welcomed. Highlighted the fact that several of the 
houses in the estate needed emergency repair works before the 
onset of winter. 
• Update sought on lands at Ballynally, Moville and in terms of 
available land for housing and housing development in Moville. 
• Housing development crucial for rural Ireland. 
• Urgent need for housing in the Milford Electoral Area including 
all associated utility services.  
• Uisce Éireann clinics are not working and there needs to be 
greater interaction. 
• More independent living facilities needed. 
• Greater emphasis needed to highlight the fact that 85% of 
houses in a Gaeltacht development should be assigned to Irish 
speakers.  

5 
 
• No reference made to the use of modular homes. 
 
Queries  
▪ Are there completion dates for the projects at Oranhill, Killylastin 
and for the Cluid Project? 
▪ What analysis is being done in terms of accessible housing and 
homes for older people especially at pre planning stage.  
▪ What is being done to address the healthcare, social and 
community needs in relation to a number of the larger projects 
and those in the Letterkenny area? 
▪ Is the Council still pursuing the acquisition of lands throughout 
the county?  
▪ Are there lands available for housing in Inishowen? 
▪ What can be done to assist the four private properties adjacent 
to the development in Dunkineely gain access to the public 
sewer? 
▪ What is happening in relation to the development in An Fál 
Carrach and is there a dedicated timeline? 
▪ Why are the costs associated with building S.I houses so high? 
▪ Given the unique situation in Donegal where are we at in terms 
of making a special case for the county in terms of the Housing 
Acquisition Programme? 
▪ Did the Council make an application in relation in respect of the 
€80m recently allocated by the Department for house 
acquisition? 
▪ Submission needs to be made to the Department regarding 
increasing the thresholds for the acquisition of S.I housing as 
this would allow more people to get on the property ladder. 
 
Mr Patsy Lafferty, Director Housing, Corporate & Cultural Services in 
response to the query on the €80m allocated nationally for house 
acquisition said that same was based on population and housing need. 
Every effort would be made, he contended, to make the case for 
Donegal considering its own unique housing circumstances. He noted 
that there were several queries on the various housing thresholds and 
that there would be an opportunity to discuss same at the Plenary 
Meeting on the 29th September. 
 
Mr Donal Walker, Senior Engineer, updated the meeting in relation to a 
number of projects in Letterkenny. 
 
It was confirmed that submissions to the Department were based on 
gross need. He also outlined the fact that the timeline for a specific 
project depended on the project itself and the scale of same with 
developments having to go through the same planning process as 
private sector projects. He noted that considerable effort had gone in to 
streamlining the process and preparing the information needed so that 
the necessary groundworks could be completed prior to construction. 
There was an acknowledgement that there had been issue with some 
of the legacy sites. Every effort was now being made, he said, in terms 

6 
 
of in-house development, design and build and turnkey projects to 
accelerate the timelines as quickly as possible. It was difficult, he 
added to provide a definitive timeline as all projects involved the use of 
public monies and contacts had to be robust to deliver projects in line 
with the public purse.  
 
Mr Walker confirmed that 30% of all Houses in Donegal had to be 
universally adapted or be readily adaptable for people with special 
needs. He said that increased thresholds had been identified for 
turnkey projects in terms of universal design to cater for special needs 
applicants.   
 
Land for housing was he stated being actively pursued with priority 
being given to buying and building on our own land to deliver housing 
in the short and long-term. 
 
It was noted that S.I Cottage applications were being prioritised as they 
came in and that seven developments were progressing at present.  
 
Work, he stated, was ongoing in Inishowen, and there was potential to 
look at smaller and larger developments. 
 
Referencing the 4 private houses adjacent to the development in 
Dunkineely, he confirmed that application to the public mains would be 
a matter for the property owners concerned but that the Council would 
assist if possible as the development proceeded. The homeowners 
concerned, he added, needed to contact Uisce Éireann directly.  
 
There were, he advised, two projects progressing in An Fál Carrach 
including AHB and turnkey project The turnkey project, it was noted, 
was projected to be on site in the first quarter of 2026 depending on the 
Uisce Éireann application.  
 
It was acknowledged that there had been ongoing engagement with 
the Department in relation to use of modular homes. 
 
€2m of an allocation, it was confirmed, had been received in 2025 for 
house acquisition and a subsequent increase of 30% provided. He 
confirmed that there was ongoing engagement with the Department in 
relation to same and with regard to buying additional units. It was he 
confirmed particularly difficult in Donegal given the defective concrete 
block issues and the need for testing.  
 
Concluding, he said, that if members had additional queries, they could 
contact the team directly. 
 
Mr John Mc Laughlin, Chief Executive said that it had been a positive 
meeting and that it was now obvious that considerable progress had 
been made. He acknowledged the fact that there were challenges 
ahead in terms of delivering over 1800 units and that it would be 

7 
 
necessary to have clear oversight of the task that was ahead. There 
were difficulties also, he noted, in terms of recruiting staff to work on 
the programme. It would be necessary, he added, for all stakeholders 
and the elected members to work collectively to progress same. He 
referenced also the fact that the Council would have to pay tax on land 
that it bought in the future, and this was detrimental to the overall 
objective. Uisce Éireann’s investment programme also needed to be 
aligned with the Council’s needs, he said, and this would necessitate a 
change in policy going forward.  
 
He welcomed the fact that the Department wanted to move to a single 
stage application process but indicated that there were conditions that 
needed to be further clarified as local authorities could end up carrying 
additional costs. Considerable efforts were being made to streamline 
the process, he stated, and to ensure that the value for money aspect 
was adhered to. 
 
He alluded to the fact that success was measured on numbers and that 
there was little or no credit for small developments that were vital for 
rural communities. Thus, a fairer reporting system was needed, he 
added.  
 
Concluding, he said, that he appreciated that there were concerns in 
relation to the average delivery times, but that cognisance had to be 
taken of the scale of development and the resources available. 
 
Cllr Mc Monagle at this juncture said that he believed there was no 
need to have a housing update every month and proposed that the 
Special Meetings could be used to focus on other issues such as the 
urban and rural regeneration projects where specific issues/barriers 
had emerged in relation to a few of these projects. This was seconded 
by Cllr Devine who indicated that there were a range of issues across 
the various services that would benefit from further discussion.  
 
The Chief Executive said that it would be beneficial for a short housing 
update to be provided going forward but that the inclusion of other 
items warranted careful consideration and inclusion in a structured 
manner.  
 
He said that he would liaise with the Cathaoirleach and that a more 
comprehensive schedule could be drawn up for 2026. 
 
This concluded the business of the meeting.
