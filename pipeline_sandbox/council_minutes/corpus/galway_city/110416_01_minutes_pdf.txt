Annual Financial Statement — year ending 31st December, 2015
Ms. E. McCormack, Head of Finance referred to the Unaudited Annual Financial Statement 2015
circulated to members and outlined the content therein. Ms. McCormack provided an overview of
the AFS, noting that:
5
The value of fixed assets has increased by €4m since 2014, primarily as a result of the acquisition of
housing units.
The steadfast efforts of the team in the Debt Management Unit are reflected in the increased
collection percentage for commercial rates and housing rents in 2015. The bad debt provision was
increased to provide for outstanding doubtful debts.
The Income and Expenditure account generated a modest surplus on of €116,237 for 2015. The
cumulative credit revenue balance at the year-end is €370,930 compared to a balance of €254,693
at 31st December 2014, thereby strengthening the financial position of the City Council
Galway City Council was allocated funding from Local Property Tax Receipts in the amount of
€6.7million and a breakdown of same in included in Appendix 9.
The Leisureland facility was re-opened in 2015 and the facility operated within the subvention
approved in Galway City Councils 2015 budget. However, the outturn for Town Hall Theatre and
Black Box facilities for 2015 resulted in an increased demand on the Council resources of €100,000
when compared with previous years.
The non-principal private residence (NPPR) charge was abolished in 2013. However, the City Council
received income of €1,063,658 from collection of outstanding liabilities in 2015.
The amount outstanding from debtors has reduced by €12.6million from 2014 and is mainly as a
result of the receipt of payment from Irish Water for the transfer to water-related assets.
Capital expenditure in 2015 amounted to €18.7million, while capital income amounted to
€12.1million. The net income and transfers to/from reserves resulted in a cumulative surplus of
€3.1million. The net surplus is reflective of the excess of reserves over adverse capital balances and
this surplus has decreased by €5.1million from 2014, mainly as a result of the utilisation of the
Rental Accommodation Scheme reserve. The adverse capital balances continue to require funding
from a provision in the Revenue budget over a number of years.
The balance due by the City Council on long-term loans at 31/12/2015 is €73.8million, compared to
€85.7million at 31/12/2014. The reduction has resulted from the repayment of bridging finance
loans in respect of affordable housing and the repayment of mortgage and non-mortgage loans.
Loans from the Housing Finance Agency in respect of Water Assets were repaid on receipt of funding
from the Department of Environment, Community & Local Government. The mortgage loan-funding
gap has been eliminated in 2015 and amounts receivable from loan customers now exceed the
amount outstanding to the Housing Finance Agency to fund such loans.
The Mayor and Councillors thanked Ms. McCormack for the report and requested clarification on a
number of issues. Questions and requests for clarification were received from Cllrs. O. Crowe, P,

Integrated Transport Management Strategy
Mayor Fahy welcomed Mr. D. McDaid from ARUP, Mr. H. Creegan Deputy Chief Executive National
Transport Authority and representatives from SYSTRA, Galway County Council and staff from the
Galway Transport Unit to the meeting to present details of the Galway Transport Strategy.
Mr. McDaid outlined a comprehensive overview of the Strategy including: Introduction; Policy and
Transport Context; Strategy Development; Traffic Network; City Public Transport; Regional Public
Transport; Cycling, Walking & Public Realm; Complimentary Measures and Implementation and
Outcomes. Copies of the presentation were distributed to Councillors. On completion of the
presentation, Mayor Fahy thanked Mr. McDaid for the overview.
10
The consultation process and modelling framework were also detailed and Mr. J. O'Neill further
advised Councillors that the Transport SPC would consider the Plan in more detail at its meeting on
the 19th April and extended an invitation to all Councillors to attend.
Contributions were received from a number of Councillors who thanked Mr. McDaid for the
presentation. Comments were made by Cllrs. D. Lyons, MJ. Crowe, D. McDonnell, C. Connolly, C. Ö
Conchüir, N. McNelis, M. Cubbard, N. Larkin, T. O'Flaherty, P. Conneely, J. Walsh and Mayor F. Fahy.
Clarifications were sought on a number of issues raised during the presentation and Councillors
noted the urgency of reaching solutions to the city's traffic problems. Queries on the data
referenced in the report and its validity were raised and clarified. Bus route options were discussed
and funding required to implement the strategy was mentioned by a number of Councillors. Specific
issues at Parkmore were highlighted by Councillors. The overall process of compiling the report and
its costs were queried, reference to taxi services noted and restrictions on HGV's, park and ride
options, river crossings, bus lanes and bus corridors, schools transport, extension of bike scheme,
plans for William St and Eglinton St, requirement for 'real time' information, upgrade requirements
for bus fleet and possibility of introducing a congestion charge were outlined.
Councillors welcomed the report while noting some concerns with its content. Implementation and
funding requirements were stated as an obvious next requirement if the Plan is to be successful. Mr.
H. Creegan NTA provided clarification on a number of queries raised including the overall cost of the
report. It was also noted in replies that the solutions proposed are evidenced based upon data from
up to date modelling. The modelling provides for projected growth over a twenty year period. The
technical data will be made available to Councillors to aid consideration. Mr. Creegan noted that
such an overall strategy was required in order to make the case for funding and that what was
presented represents a coherent all encompassing plan. Mr. J. Molloy SEE provided further
clarifications on a number of issues raised including proposals for Parkmore and timescales for same.
Mr. J. O'Neill noted that Councillors require time to consider the Plan and thanked the Deputy Chief
Executive of the NTA, Mr. Creegan for attending the meeting and support in the preparation of the
Plan. Mr. O'Neill outlined the consultation process scheduled over the coming weeks with local
interest groups and stakeholders, including Access for All, Chamber of Commerce, Galway City
Business Association, Latin Quarter, Westend, and Galway Village East traders groups. The
timescales for consideration of the strategy in line with the review of the City Development Plan was
also discussed and Mr. O'Neill advised that the Council is examining what amendments to the Draft
Plan may be required.
Chief Executive Mr. McGrath reiterated the importance of the strategy for the future development
and growth of Galway city and region and noted that it was one of the most importance documents
to come before Council in recent years. He also observed that the content of the strategy is very new
and very different and the totality of options have been considered in the strategy. He advised
Councillors to be considered and supportive of the strategy. In the context of alleviating the city's
transport issues and challenges, doing nothing is not an option. He advised Councillors that all data
gathered in the preparation of the Plan will be made available and noted that the modelling used
throughout the Plan was comprehensive. He noted that some of the various measures proposed
would require approval of members through the Part 8 Process.
