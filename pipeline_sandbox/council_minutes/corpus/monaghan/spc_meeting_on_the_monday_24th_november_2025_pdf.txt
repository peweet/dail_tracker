2. Presentation from Dr Patricia McCreesh, on “Tree and Hedgerow Strategy for Monaghan County 
Council”. 
Dr. Patricia McCreesh gave a comprehensive presentation to members regarding the Tree and 
Hedgerow Strategy for Monaghan County Council. Dr. Patricia McCreesh informed the members the 
strategy provides a clear vision for managing trees and hedgerows across the county, outlining 
objectives and measurable actions to support sustainable tree care, biodiversity, and climate 
resilience. Dr. Patricia McCreesh spoke about how the draft aims to protect existing treescapes on 
both public and private land, increase canopy cover, and improve species diversity while engaging 
communities and stakeholders through education and involvement. Dr. P. McCreesh advised the 
strategy aligns with national and local planning policies and addresses legal obligations, risk 
management, and best practices in tree care. Dr. McCreesh stated Monaghan’s landscape includes 
over 12,845 km of hedgerows with 37 species, a canopy cover of approximately 17%, and a strong 
cultural and historical connection to trees. 
Dr. P. McCreesh advised the strategy also considers challenges such as climate change, 
population growth, development pressures, ash dieback, pests, diseases, and unauthorised works.  Dr. 
Patricia McCreesh spoke about how the draft emphasises compiling a tree inventory, planning for 
future needs, and integrating arboricultural considerations into development processes from initial 
design to construction supervision and tree protection. Dr. McCreesh stated by adopting this 
approach, Monaghan County Council seeks to safeguard its treescape, enhance environmental 
beneﬁts, and ensure compliance with planning and legislative requirements while fostering 
collaboration with communities and partner organisations.  Dr. McCreesh reported that by compiling 
an inventory of trees across the county, the council can better understand the treescape and help to 
identify works and develop a tree care program. In addition, Dr. McCreesh stated that it would be 
beneﬁcial to secure funding for a tree oﬃcer for the county. 
Cllr. R.  Truell thanked Dr. McCreesh for her presentation and invited questions from 
members. Dr. McCreesh addressed inquiries concerning the removal of trees aﬀected by ash dieback 
along the roads in the Monaghan area, as well as the potential for grant funding in Monaghan for the 
removal of these trees. N. O’Connor (DOS) recommended reaching out to Leitrim County Council to 
inquire about their tree funding grants. Dr. P. McCreesh indicated that the Tree and Hedgerow 
Strategy will undergo public consultation during the upcoming four weeks. 
 

3.  Presentation from Dr Patricia McCreesh, Biodiversity Oﬃcer, on “Biodiversity Action Plan 2025-
2030”. 
Dr. Patricia McCreesh gave a detailed presentation to members on the Monaghan Biodiversity Action 
Plan that outlines a framework to conserve and enhance biodiversity across the county. Dr. McCreesh 
reported that the structure is around four key themes: knowledge and research, conservation and 
restoration, governance and leadership, and connecting to nature. Dr. Patricia McCreesh discussed 
the importance of the plan in emphasising the management of invasive species, strategies for trees 
and hedgerows, and the centralization of biodiversity data to facilitate informed decision-making. Dr. 
Patricia McCreesh stated that the development of a biodiversity guidance document for MCC sections 
engaged in planning, design, and construction aims to offer practical, locally pertinent guidance on 
achieving biodiversity-positive results, emphasising habitat protection, species conservation, and net 
biodiversity gain. Dr. Patricia McCreesh spoke about the collaboration with the MCC Climate Action 
Team in delivering biodiversity actions from the Climate Action Plan (2024–2029). Climate and 
biodiversity deliver co-benefits that enhance ecosystem resilience, support carbon sequestration, and 
improve community well-being. Dr. P. McCreesh advised the possibility for the establishment of an 
MCC ‘Green Team’ of volunteer staff that are interested in promoting biodiversity, environmental, 
climate & sustainability issues. 
Dr. McCreesh reported further prioritising the focus on restoring ecosystems, supporting 
conservation projects like Sliabh Beagh SAC and wetlands, promoting biodiversity-friendly land 
management, and implementing national frameworks such as the All-Ireland Pollinator Plan. Dr. 
Patricia McCreesh spoke about how the governance measures include embedding biodiversity into 
MCC policies, reducing pesticide use, piloting nature-based solutions, and ensuring compliance with 
national and EU legislation. Dr. P. McCreesh advised the progress will be tracked through clear KPIs 
such as audits completed, habitats restored, species monitored, and participation levels in community 
projects. Dr. McCreesh states that community engagement is central, with initiatives for education, 
citizen science, local action plans, and biodiversity conferences to foster awareness and stewardship. 
Dr. McCreesh responded to inquiries concerning the ancient woodland located in the 
Monaghan region and the alternatives to peat. Dr. McCreesh indicated that the ancient woodland in 
the Monaghan area is not under the ownership of the council; it is privately held. Dr. McCreesh noted 
that this situation can present challenges; however, it is possible to enhance public awareness of 
biodiversity by organising free-to-attend biodiversity events and training sessions. The motion for the 
Biodiversity Action Plan 2025-2030 was proposed by L. Cunnigham and seconded by B. McAree. 
 
4. Update from Shane Treanor, on Phase 2 of the Community Climate Action Programme. 
S. Treanor gave a presentation to members regarding the second round of the Community Climate 
Action Programme, which opened for applications on the 28th of October 2025 and will close on the 
6th of January 2026. S. Treanor reported the Monaghan County Council has received €392,000 in 
funding for Phase 2, Strand 1, with criteria remaining unchanged from Phase 1. S. Treanor advised that 
the Community Climate Action Oﬃcer is actively supporting local groups through social media 
campaigns, meetings, and drop-in clinics scheduled for the 28th of November in Clones Library, the 
1st of December in Carrickmacross Library, and the 2nd of December in Monaghan Town Library. S. 
Treanor stated that there is already a good mix of potential applications from the three MDs, but 
everyone is encouraged to share this funding opportunity within their networks. 
L. Cunningham inquired whether the Water Schemes are eligible to apply for funding. S. 
Treanor and N. O’Connor responded that they would investigate the potential for funding for the 
water schemes. 

5. Update from Niall Malone, Administrative Oﬃcer on the “Environment, Biodiversity & Circular 
Economy Fund 2026”. 
N. Malone gave a presentation to members regarding the Monaghan County Council’s Environment, 
Biodiversity, and Circular Economy Fund, which is now open for applications, with a closing date of 
Friday, 9 January 2026, to allow early decisions for planning purposes. N. Malone stated that eligible 
applicants include community and voluntary groups, organisations, and residents’ committees within 
County Monaghan, provided they are registered with the Monaghan Public Participation Network. N. 
Malone notiﬁed the members that only one application per group is allowed and that the application 
process is entirely online this year. N. Malone spoke about how the projects may include maintenance 
activities such as litter control and upkeep of public spaces; environmental improvements like street 
furniture; and biodiversity initiatives such as planting native species, creating hedgerows, installing 
bat and bird boxes, and developing biodiversity action plans. In addition, N. Malone stated other 
projects include hosting educational events, circular economy projects including reuse and repair 
schemes, upcycling and waste prevention campaigns. 
  
Cllr. R. Truell thanked N. Malone for his presentation and invited questions from members. N. 
Malone addressed inquiries regarding the total funding amount available in comparison to the 
previous year. 
 
6. Update from Niall Malone, Administrative Oﬃcer on “Monaghan County Council's Litter 
Management Plan 2025-2028.” 
N. Malone gave a detailed presentation to members regarding the Litter Management Plan, which 
sets out Monaghan County Council’s strategy to prevent and control litter across the county. N. 
Malone stated that the plan builds on previous progress and emphasises shared responsibility among 
residents, businesses, and visitors. N. Malone reported the plan includes education and awareness 
campaigns, community engagement through initiatives like Green Schools, Picker Pals, and Tidy 
Towns, as well as enforcement measures such as ﬁnes, CCTV monitoring, and household waste 
compliance checks. N. Malone spoke about how the plan addresses speciﬁc challenges like dog fouling, 
cigarette and vaping litter, illegal dumping, and litter from convenience food outlets, while promoting 
sustainable practices through schemes like Deposit Return, green dog walker scheme, national spring 
clean, Monaghan clean up and “Green Your Festival.” 
N. Malone informed the members of a new project that the waste team completed in 
September. N. Malone advised that the project focuses on investigating the people in Monaghan to 
see if they have a registered bin, and if not, we will be sending legal notices. N. Malone spoke about 
how the plan outlines the actions under four pillars: education and awareness, partnership and 
community support, local authority prevention and control, and enforcement and regulation. N. 
Malone stated the key measures include supporting cleanup campaigns, maintaining street cleaning 
schedules, servicing litter bins, and implementing anti-dumping initiatives. N. Malone reported that 
the progress will be monitored through annual reviews, litter surveys, and key performance indicators 
such as complaint numbers, ﬁnes issued, and Tidy Towns results. In addition, N. Malone informed the 
members that the plan concludes by emphasising the collaboration among stakeholders and fostering 
a culture of responsible waste management to keep Monaghan clean, attractive, and environmentally 
sustainable. N. Malone announced that the Litter Management Plan is scheduled for public 
consultation in Qtr 1 of 2026. 
Cllr. R. Truell thanked N. Malone for his presentation and invited questions from members. N. 
Malone addressed inquiries concerning CCTV, litter ﬁnes, GDPR, say it APP, recycling events, and the 
water system in Rossmore Park. Additional questions pertained to registered bins in the area, and the 
procedure for submitting litter complaints to the council. K. Duﬀy informed the members that he is 

currently working on creating complaints form on the council's website for public use.  The motion for 
the Litter Management Plan 2025-2028 was proposed by B. McAree and seconded by R. Truell.
